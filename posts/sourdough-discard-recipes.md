# 8 Ways to Use Sourdough Discard

Every time you feed your starter, you pour off a portion before adding fresh flour and water. That discarded starter is called — predictably — discard. And most beginners throw it straight in the trash.

Stop doing that.

Sourdough discard is packed with wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria. It won't leaven bread on its own (it's past its peak), but it adds incredible tangy flavor and tender texture to everything from pancakes to pizza dough. These are the recipes our readers make most.

---

## 1. Sourdough Discard Pancakes

The quickest, most satisfying use of discard. These are fluffy, tangy, and ready in 20 minutes.

**Ingredients (serves 2–3):**
- 1 cup (240g) sourdough discard
- 1 egg
- 1 tbsp butter, melted
- 1 tbsp sugar
- ½ tsp baking soda
- ½ tsp salt
- 2 tbsp milk (if needed for consistency)

**Method:** Whisk everything together. Cook on a medium-hot buttered skillet, 2–3 minutes per side. Don't overcrowd the pan.

The baking soda reacts with the acid in the discard and gives you lift. These are genuinely better than regular pancakes.

---

## 2. Discard Crackers

Crispy, addictive, and a great vehicle for cheese and charcuterie.

**Ingredients:**
- 1 cup (240g) discard
- ¼ cup olive oil
- ½ tsp salt
- Optional: rosemary, everything bagel seasoning, parmesan

**Method:** Mix discard, olive oil, and salt. Spread very thin on a parchment-lined baking sheet. Season. Bake at 350°F for 25–35 minutes until golden and crisp. Break into shards when cool.

---

## 3. Sourdough Banana Bread

The tang of the discard pairs perfectly with ripe bananas. Moist, flavorful, and dangerously easy.

**Ingredients:**
- 3 very ripe bananas, mashed
- ½ cup (120g) discard
- ⅓ cup melted butter
- ¾ cup sugar
- 1 egg
- 1 tsp vanilla
- 1 tsp baking soda
- Pinch of salt
- 1 ½ cups all-purpose flour

**Method:** Mix wet ingredients, fold in dry ingredients, pour into a greased loaf pan. Bake at 350°F for 55–65 minutes until a toothpick comes out clean.

---

## 4. Pizza Dough

Discard pizza dough is chewier and more flavorful than standard pizza dough. Use it same-day or refrigerate for up to 3 days for even better flavor.

**Ingredients (2 pizzas):**
- 200g discard
- 250g bread flour
- 150g warm water
- 1 tsp active dry yeast (yes, commercial yeast — this isn't a leavening recipe)
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tbsp olive oil

**Method:** Mix, knead 5 minutes, rest 1 hour at room temp, refrigerate overnight. Shape cold, top, and bake at your oven's max temp on a preheated pizza stone or steel.

---

## 5. Discard Waffles

Same base as the pancakes but crispier exterior, especially good in a Belgian waffle iron.

Substitute 1 cup discard for 1 cup flour + ½ cup buttermilk in your favorite waffle recipe. Add ¼ tsp baking soda to the batter. The results speak for themselves.

---

## 6. Sourdough Flatbreads

Mix 1 cup discard with a pinch of salt. Cook in a dry cast iron pan over high heat, 1–2 minutes per side until charred spots appear. Brush with garlic butter. Done.

These take 10 minutes total and taste like something from a wood-fired restaurant.

---

## 7. Sourdough Muffins

Base formula for discard muffins:
- 1 cup discard
- 1 egg
- ¼ cup oil or melted butter
- ¼ cup sugar or honey
- 1 cup flour
- 1 tsp baking powder
- ½ tsp baking soda
- Pinch salt
- Any mix-ins: blueberries, chocolate chips, nuts

Fold gently, fill muffin cups ¾ full, bake at 375°F for 18–22 minutes.

---

## 8. Sourdough English Muffins

More involved than the others, but produces the best English muffins you've ever had — full of nooks and crannies, tangy, and perfect toasted.

**Ingredients:**
- 200g discard
- 300g bread flour
- 150g warm milk
- 1 tsp dry yeast
- 1 tbsp sugar
- 1 tsp salt
- Cornmeal for dusting

**Method:** Mix and knead, rise 1–2 hours, cut into rounds, rest 30 minutes, cook in a dry pan on low heat covered, 6–7 minutes per side. Split with a fork, never a knife.

---

## Storing Your Discard

Keep discard in a jar in the fridge for up to 2 weeks. Every time you feed your starter, add to it rather than throwing it out. When you have enough for a recipe, use it.

The best discard recipes use older, more sour discard — don't feel pressured to use it immediately. The tang is the whole point.
