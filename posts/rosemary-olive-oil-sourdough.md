# Rosemary Olive Oil Sourdough

This is the loaf that disappears fastest at any gathering. The rosemary perfumes the whole kitchen as it bakes, the olive oil enriches the crumb and deepens the crust color, and the combination makes something that tastes like it came from an Italian bakery.

It pairs with almost everything: torn and dipped in more olive oil, served with aged parmesan, alongside roasted vegetables, or simply toasted with butter. It's also one of the easiest flavored sourdoughs — no add-ins to incorporate carefully, just a few simple additions to the base dough.

## Ingredients

- **450g** bread flour
- **310g** water, room temperature
- **90g** active sourdough starter
- **9g** fine sea salt
- **40g** good quality extra virgin olive oil (this is flavor — don't use cheap oil)
- **2 tbsp** fresh rosemary, finely chopped (about 3 large sprigs)
- **1 tsp** flaky sea salt for the top (optional but recommended)

**Yield:** 1 loaf | **Total time:** 24–36 hours

---

## Notes on Olive Oil in Bread

Fat slows fermentation by coating the yeast cells and reducing their activity. This means:

1. Your bulk fermentation will take longer than a plain sourdough — plan for 6–10 hours at 72°F
2. The crumb will be slightly more tender and fine than a lean sourdough — this is not a bug, it's a feature
3. The crust will brown more deeply and quickly — watch your bake time on the uncovered phase

---

## Method

### 1. Autolyse (30 minutes)
Mix flour and water until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest 30 minutes.

### 2. Add Remaining Ingredients
Add starter, salt, olive oil, and rosemary. Use your hands to pinch and squeeze everything together until fully incorporated. The dough will feel slightly slicker than a lean dough — that's the olive oil.

Rest covered for 30 minutes.

### 3. Stretch and Folds
Perform **4 sets of stretch and folds** over 2 hours, spaced 30 minutes apart. The olive oil means the dough will feel smooth and slightly less sticky than usual. Enjoy it — it handles nicely.

### 4. Bulk Fermentation
Leave at room temperature until the dough has risen **50–75%** and shows all the usual signs of readiness (jiggle, bubbles, domed top). Given the olive oil, budget extra time: **6–10 hours** at 72°F.

> **Pro tip:** The rosemary aroma will intensify throughout bulk fermentation. By hour 4 or 5, your kitchen will smell incredible. This is normal and good.

### 5. Shape
Turn onto a lightly floured surface and shape as you normally would. The olive oil makes this dough very cooperative — good news if you struggle with shaping.

Place seam-side up in a floured banneton.

### 6. Cold Proof
Refrigerate **10–16 hours**. The overnight cold proof lets the olive oil flavor fully integrate throughout the dough.

### 7. Bake

Preheat oven and Dutch oven to **500°F (260°C)** for 45 minutes.

Turn out cold dough onto parchment. Score with a bold slash or a design if you're feeling confident — rosemary sourdough is a great loaf to practice decorative scoring on since the crust color is so striking.

Before baking, you can press a few extra rosemary sprigs and a pinch of flaky salt onto the scored surface.

- **Covered:** 20 minutes at 500°F
- **Uncovered:** 18–22 minutes at 450°F

**Watch carefully on the uncovered phase.** The olive oil causes deeper, faster browning. Pull it when it's dark golden — not pale, not burnt. The olive oil crust should look deeply golden, almost lacquered.

### 8. Cool
Rest 1 hour before cutting. The olive oil crumb needs time to set properly.

## Variations

**Garlic rosemary:** Add 4 roasted garlic cloves (squeezed from their skins) during the stretch and fold phase. Roasted garlic sweetens and softens — raw garlic would be too sharp.

**Lemon rosemary:** Add zest of 1 lemon with the rosemary. The lemon brightens the whole loaf and pairs especially well with fish dishes.

**Olive rosemary:** Fold in 80g of roughly chopped Kalamata or Castelvetrano olives during lamination. Use the olive brine as part of your water measurement (replace 30g of water with olive brine for extra depth).

**Herbes de Provence:** Replace rosemary with 1 tbsp herbes de Provence for a more complex, floral herb profile.

---

## Serving Suggestions

- Torn into chunks and served with a good olive oil for dipping, plus aged parmesan
- Alongside a cheese board — pairs especially well with manchego, pecorino, and brie
- With roasted chicken or lamb
- Toasted and rubbed with a cut garlic clove while hot, then drizzled with olive oil (*pan con tomate* style)
- As the bread in a very good bruschetta

The leftover crumbs, collected and toasted, make outstanding croutons for Caesar salad or Italian wedding soup.
