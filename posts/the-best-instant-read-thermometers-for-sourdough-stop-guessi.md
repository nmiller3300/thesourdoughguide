## Why Temperature Matters in Sourdough

Temperature controls nearly everything in bread baking. Your water temperature determines your final dough temperature. Your dough temperature determines fermentation speed. And your internal bread temperature tells you, definitively, when your loaf is done.

Without a thermometer, you're making educated guesses. With one, you're making informed decisions.

### The Three Critical Measurements

**Water Temperature for Mixing**

When you're calculating desired dough temperature (and you should be), you need to know exactly how warm your water is. The difference between 85°F and 95°F water can mean the difference between a perfectly fermented loaf and an overproofed mess.

**Dough Temperature After Mixing**

Checking your dough temperature immediately after mixing tells you how your fermentation will proceed. Hit your target of 75-78°F, and you can follow timing guidelines confidently. Come in at 82°F, and you know to watch your dough more carefully.

**Internal Bread Temperature**

This is the measurement that changed my baking life. A fully baked sourdough loaf reaches an internal temperature of 205-210°F. Below 200°F, and you'll likely have a gummy crumb. Above 212°F, and you're pushing toward over-baked.

No amount of tapping the bottom or checking crust color gives you this precision.

## What Makes a Good Thermometer for Baking

### Speed

You want a reading in 2-3 seconds, not 10-15. When you're checking bread in a hot oven, every second matters. You're losing heat, and you're risking burns.

### Accuracy

Look for thermometers accurate to within ±1°F. Cheaper models might be off by 3-5 degrees, which matters when you're targeting specific temperatures.

### Probe Length

For bread baking, you need a probe long enough to reach the center of your loaf — at least 4 inches. Shorter probes work for steaks but leave you guessing with a thick boule.

### Temperature Range

Most baking applications fall between 32°F and 212°F, which any decent thermometer handles. But if you also roast meat or make candy, look for a wider range.

### Durability

You'll be using this near hot ovens, in steamy kitchens, with flour-covered hands. It needs to survive real-world conditions.

## How to Use Your Thermometer for Bread

### Checking Water Temperature

Simple: submerge the probe, wait for the reading to stabilize, adjust your water temperature as needed. I keep a kettle of hot water and add cold water until I hit my target.

### Checking Dough Temperature

Insert the probe into the center of your dough immediately after mixing. Take 2-3 readings in different spots and average them. The center is usually warmest from friction during mixing.

### Checking Bread Doneness

Insert the probe into the bottom-center of your loaf, angling toward the middle. The bottom is thickest and slowest to bake, so if it's at 205°F, your loaf is done. Avoid inserting through the top crust — you'll mar the appearance.
