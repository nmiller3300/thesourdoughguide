# Sourdough Starter Not Rising?

A starter that won't rise is the most common problem beginners face — and almost always fixable. Before you throw it out and start over, work through this diagnostic guide. The cause is almost certainly one of the six issues below.

## How to Tell If Your Starter Is Actually Failing

First, establish a baseline. Feed your starter at a consistent time (say, 8am), then mark the jar with a rubber band at the current level. Check every hour for 8 hours.

**Healthy behavior:** Doubles within 4–8 hours, smells pleasantly tangy, falls back down after peaking.

**Problem behavior:** Barely rises, doesn't double, smells off, or shows no activity after 8 hours.

---

## Cause 1: Your Kitchen Is Too Cold

This is the most common cause of a sluggish starter. Wild yeast becomes nearly dormant below 65°F (18°C).

**Signs:** Starter shows some bubbles but very slow to rise. Activity picks up in warmer parts of the day.

**The fix:**
- Find a warm spot — top of the refrigerator, inside the oven with just the light on (~78°F), or near a heating vent
- Aim for 75–80°F (24–27°C) for your starter
- Use warm water (80–85°F) when feeding
- In winter, consider a proofing box or a small cooler with a jar of hot water

> **Quick test:** Feed your starter and place it on a heating pad set to low. If it rises noticeably faster, temperature was your problem.

---

## Cause 2: Your Water Has Chlorine

Chlorine and chloramines in municipal tap water can suppress or kill the bacteria and wild yeast in your starter.

**Signs:** Starter bubbles minimally, never rises significantly, despite warm temperatures.

**The fix:**
- Use filtered water (Brita filter removes chlorine)
- Use bottled spring water
- Leave tap water in an open container overnight — chlorine dissipates, though chloramines do not
- Well water works great if available

Switch to filtered water for one week and feed daily. Most starters recover quickly.

---

## Cause 3: Your Starter Is Too Young

A brand-new starter takes 7–14 days to establish a strong microbial community. Expecting a 3-day-old starter to double reliably is unrealistic.

**Signs:** You started from scratch less than 2 weeks ago. Activity is inconsistent — rises some days, barely at all others.

**The fix:**
- Keep going. Feed daily at the same time.
- Add a tablespoon of whole wheat or rye flour to your next few feedings — it's loaded with wild yeast and bacteria that accelerate establishment
- Be patient. A properly established starter is worth waiting for.

---

## Cause 4: The Wrong Flour

All-purpose flour works, but whole grain flours establish starters faster and maintain them more vigorously. Very refined white flour can produce a sluggish starter.

**Signs:** You've been using bleached all-purpose flour exclusively. Activity is low despite good temperature.

**The fix:**
- Replace 20–30% of your flour with whole wheat or rye for a week
- You'll notice significantly more activity within 2–3 feedings
- Once established, you can transition back to mostly white flour if preferred

---

## Cause 5: Your Starter Is Over-Acidic

If you haven't fed your starter in a while, it becomes extremely acidic. At high acidity, the environment becomes hostile even to the microbes that live there.

**Signs:** Strong vinegar or acetone smell. Liquid layer (hooch) on top. Has been in the fridge for weeks without feeding.

**The fix:**
1. Pour off all but 10g of starter
2. Feed with fresh flour and water (1:5:5 ratio — 10g starter : 50g flour : 50g water)
3. Leave at room temperature
4. Repeat this feeding every 12 hours for 3–5 days
5. Gradually reduce the ratio back to 1:1:1 as it recovers

This "rehabilitation" process reduces acidity and lets the healthy organisms reestablish dominance.

---

## Cause 6: You're Feeding at the Wrong Time

Many bakers use their starter too early (before peak) or too late (after it has peaked and collapsed). Both produce poor results.

**Signs:** You're using starter that has collapsed or that hasn't fully risen yet.

**The fix:** Use your starter at peak — when it has roughly doubled, looks domed on top, is full of bubbles, and the edges haven't yet started to recede. This is when yeast activity is highest.

Mark your jar after feeding and watch when it peaks. This timing is your baking window.

---

## The Nuclear Option: Starting Over

If after 2 weeks of dedicated daily feedings with warm temperatures and filtered water your starter still shows no meaningful activity, it's worth starting fresh. Occasionally a starter establishes with a bacterial community that crowds out the wild yeast.

Starting over isn't failure — it's data. Use whole wheat flour and filtered water from day one, keep it at 78°F consistently, and you'll have an active starter within a week.

---

## Quick Diagnostic Checklist

- [ ] Temperature above 75°F during feedings?
- [ ] Using filtered or bottled water?
- [ ] Starter older than 10 days?
- [ ] Using whole grain flour (at least partially)?
- [ ] Fed within the last 24 hours?
- [ ] Using at peak (doubled, domed, bubbly)?

If all six are yes and it still won't rise, start fresh. You've done everything right and sometimes it just doesn't work out.
