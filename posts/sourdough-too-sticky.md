# Sourdough Dough Too Sticky? Here's How to Handle It

Sticky sourdough dough is not broken dough. High-hydration doughs are supposed to be sticky — that moisture is what creates the open crumb and chewy texture you're after. The problem isn't the dough; it's usually technique.

Here's how to handle sticky dough at every stage.

## Why High-Hydration Dough Is Sticky

Sourdough at 70–80% hydration contains more water relative to flour than most bread doughs. That extra moisture makes the dough extensible and produces an open crumb — but it also means you need to handle it differently than a stiff sandwich loaf.

The most important mindset shift: **stop reaching for more flour.** Dusting sticky dough with extra flour disrupts your hydration ratio, changes the crust texture, and makes the dough even harder to handle long-term (the added flour doesn't distribute evenly). The solutions to sticky dough are almost never "add more flour."

---

## At Mixing Time

**What you'll see:** Dough looks shaggy, rough, and sticks to everything.

This is completely normal at the mixing stage. Gluten hasn't developed yet and the dough hasn't had time to absorb all the water. 

- Mix until no dry flour remains, then cover and rest 30 minutes (autolyse). The texture will improve dramatically with rest.
- Use a dough scraper to clean the bowl and your hands, not more flour.

---

## During Stretch and Folds

**What you'll see:** Dough sticks to your hands during folding.

- Wet your hands before each set of stretch and folds. A small bowl of water nearby makes this easy.
- Use the "slap and fold" technique on the counter if it's very slack — it develops gluten faster than bowl folds.
- Each set of stretch and folds should make the dough noticeably less sticky as gluten develops.

By the 4th set, even very high hydration doughs should feel considerably more structured and easier to handle.

---

## During Shaping

**What you'll see:** Dough sticks to the counter and your hands, won't hold shape.

This is where most people struggle. Here's what actually works:

1. **Refrigerate before shaping.** Cold dough is dramatically easier to handle. If the dough is too sticky to shape, put it in the fridge for 20–30 minutes first.
2. **Use a lightly damp bench surface, not a floured one.** Slightly damp counters create tension during shaping rather than letting the dough slide.
3. **Keep a bench scraper in your dominant hand.** Use it to fold and move the dough without touching it directly.
4. **Move quickly and confidently.** Hesitation lets the dough relax into the counter. A quick, decisive shape holds better.
5. **Build surface tension.** Drag the shaped boule toward you on an unfloured surface with cupped hands — you should feel it tighten.

---

## Long-Term Fix: Build Gluten Properly

The real solution to sticky dough is gluten development, not flour. Strong gluten holds moisture in the dough structure rather than letting it seep out onto your hands and the counter.

- Complete all 4–5 sets of stretch and folds during bulk
- Don't rush the process — each fold adds structure
- Autolyse (resting flour and water before adding starter) dramatically improves extensibility and reduces stickiness

If your dough is still extremely sticky after completing all folds and refrigerating before shaping, you can reduce hydration by 3–5% on your next bake. But try the technique fixes first — they usually solve the problem.

---

## The Essential Tool: A Bench Scraper

If you're handling high-hydration sourdough without a bench scraper, get one. It costs about $8, and it makes the difference between struggling with sticky dough and having complete control over it. Use it to fold, divide, and move dough without touching it directly. It's the single most useful tool in sourdough baking after a Dutch oven and a kitchen scale.
