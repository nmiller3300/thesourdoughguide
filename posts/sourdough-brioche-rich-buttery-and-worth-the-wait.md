There's something almost magical about brioche. That golden, pillowy crumb. The way it tears apart in soft, buttery layers. The richness that makes every bite feel like a celebration.

Now imagine that same indulgent bread with the depth and complexity that only sourdough can bring. That's what we're making today — a brioche that's tender, rich, and subtly tangy in all the right ways.

Yes, this bread takes time. Yes, it requires patience. But every single minute is worth it when you pull that burnished, golden loaf from your oven and breathe in that impossibly good aroma.

## Why Sourdough Brioche Is Special

Traditional brioche relies on commercial yeast for a quick rise. It's delicious, but it can taste one-dimensional — all butter and eggs without much complexity.

Sourdough brioche is different. The long fermentation develops flavors you simply can't achieve with instant yeast. You get subtle tang that cuts through the richness, a more tender crumb from the extended gluten development, and a depth of flavor that makes this bread genuinely unforgettable.

The sourdough also extends shelf life naturally. Where regular brioche stales within a day or two, sourdough brioche stays soft and fresh for several days longer.

## Yield

One 9x5 inch loaf or 12 brioche rolls

## Ingredients

### For the Dough

| Ingredient | Grams | Volume |
|------------|-------|--------|
| Bread flour | 400g | 3¼ cups |
| Active sourdough starter (100% hydration) | 100g | ½ cup |
| Whole milk (room temperature) | 80g | ⅓ cup |
| Large eggs (room temperature) | 150g | 3 eggs |
| Granulated sugar | 50g | ¼ cup |
| Fine sea salt | 8g | 1½ teaspoons |
| Unsalted butter (softened but still cool) | 170g | ¾ cup (1½ sticks) |

### For the Egg Wash

| Ingredient | Grams | Volume |
|------------|-------|--------|
| Egg yolk | 1 | 1 |
| Heavy cream | 15g | 1 tablespoon |
| Pinch of salt | — | — |

## Equipment Needed

- Stand mixer with dough hook (highly recommended)
- 9x5 inch loaf pan or 12-cup muffin tin
- Plastic wrap or reusable bowl cover
- Pastry brush

## Step-by-Step Method

### Day One: Mix and Bulk Ferment

**Step 1: Combine the initial dough (10 minutes)**

In the bowl of your stand mixer, combine the flour, sourdough starter, milk, eggs, and sugar. Mix on low speed with the dough hook until everything comes together into a shaggy mass, about 3-4 minutes.

Cover the bowl and let it rest for 30 minutes. This brief autolyse helps the flour hydrate and makes the next steps easier.

**Step 2: Add salt and develop gluten (15-20 minutes)**

Add the salt to the dough. Mix on medium-low speed for 10-15 minutes. The dough will look rough at first, then gradually become smoother and start pulling away from the sides of the bowl.

You're looking for a dough that's smooth, elastic, and passes the windowpane test — when you stretch a small piece, it should stretch thin enough to see light through without tearing.

**Step 3: Incorporate the butter (15-20 minutes)**

This is where patience matters most. Cut your softened butter into tablespoon-sized pieces. With the mixer on medium-low, add the butter one piece at a time, waiting until each piece is mostly incorporated before adding the next.

The dough will look like a broken, greasy mess around the halfway point. Don't panic — this is completely normal. Keep mixing.

After all the butter is added, continue mixing for another 5-8 minutes until the dough is smooth, shiny, and pulls completely away from the bowl. It should be soft and slightly sticky but hold together beautifully.

**Step 4: Bulk fermentation at room temperature (3-4 hours)**

Transfer the dough to a lightly greased container. Cover and let it rise at room temperature (70-75°F / 21-24°C) for 3-4 hours. The dough should look puffy and increase in size by about 50%.

During this time, perform one set of stretch and folds after the first hour to strengthen the dough.

**Step 5: Cold retard overnight (8-16 hours)**

Punch down the dough gently to release the gas. Cover tightly with plastic wrap, pressing it directly against the surface of the dough. Refrigerate for at least 8 hours or up to 16 hours.

This cold fermentation is essential — it develops flavor and makes the butter-rich dough much easier to handle.

### Day Two: Shape, Proof, and Bake

**Step 6: Shape the brioche (15 minutes)**

Remove the dough from the refrigerator. It will be firm and easy to work with — this is exactly what you want.

**For a loaf:** Divide the dough into 4 equal pieces (about 240g each). Roll each piece into a smooth ball, then arrange them in a row inside a greased 9x5 inch loaf pan.

**For rolls:** Divide the dough into 12 equal pieces (about 80g each). Roll each piece into a tight ball and place in a greased 12-cup muffin tin.

**Step 7: Final proof (3-5 hours)**

Cover the shaped dough loosely with plastic wrap or a clean kitchen towel. Let it proof at room temperature until the dough has nearly doubled and feels puffy and light when gently poked. For a loaf, the dough should rise about an inch above the rim of the pan.

This can take anywhere from 3-5 hours depending on your room temperature and how active your starter is. Don't rush this step — underproofed brioche will be dense and heavy.

**Step 8: Preheat and prepare egg wash**

About 30 minutes before baking, preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C).

Whisk together the egg yolk, heavy cream, and pinch of salt until smooth. This egg wash creates that gorgeous, glossy, deep golden crust.

**Step 9: Apply egg wash and bake (25-35 minutes)**

Gently brush the proofed dough with egg wash, taking care not to deflate it. Be generous but avoid letting pools collect in the crevices.

Bake until deeply golden brown and the internal temperature reaches 190°F (88°C):
- **Loaf:** 30-35 minutes
- **Rolls:** 22-28 minutes

If the top browns too quickly, tent loosely with foil for the remaining bake time.

**Step 10: Cool completely**

Remove from the pan immediately and let cool on a wire rack for at least 30 minutes before slicing. The interior continues cooking as it cools, and cutting too early will result in a gummy texture.

## Baker's Tips

**Temperature is everything with brioche.** The butter should be soft enough to incorporate but not melted or greasy. The eggs and milk should be at room temperature so they don't shock the butter and cause it to seize up. And the overnight cold fermentation isn't optional — it's what makes this dough workable.

**Don't skimp on mixing time.** Brioche dough needs extensive gluten development to support all that butter. Undermixed dough will be dense and heavy rather than light and feathery.

**Trust the windowpane test.** Before adding butter, your dough should stretch paper-thin without tearing. After adding butter, it should be even more extensible and smooth.

## Storage Instructions

Sourdough brioche keeps remarkably well thanks to the long fermentation.

**Room temperature:** Store in an airtight container or bread bag for up to 4 days. The crust will soften but the crumb stays tender.

**Freezer:** Wrap individual slices or rolls tightly in plastic wrap, then place in a freezer bag. Freeze for up to 2 months. Thaw at room temperature or warm briefly in a 300°F oven.

**Refreshing:** Day-old brioche toasts beautifully. A quick turn in a warm oven (300°F for 5 minutes) also revives it wonderfully.

## Variations

### Chocolate Chip Brioche

Fold 150g (1 cup) of chocolate chips into the dough after the butter is fully incorporated. The cold chocolate chips will stay intact better if you add them right before refrigerating.

### Orange Cardamom Brioche

Add the zest of 2 oranges and 1 teaspoon of ground cardamom to the dough along with the salt. The citrus and spice complement the buttery richness beautifully.

### Brioche Hamburger Buns

Divide the dough into 8 pieces (about 120g each). Shape into balls and flatten slightly. Proof, egg wash, and sprinkle with sesame seeds before baking at 375°F for 18-22 minutes. These buns will change your burger game forever.

### Cinnamon Sugar Pull-Apart Brioche

Roll the chilled dough into a rectangle. Brush with melted butter and sprinkle generously with cinnamon sugar. Cut into strips, stack them, and place in a loaf pan. Proof and bake as directed for an irresistible pull-apart loaf.

## The Bottom Line

Sourdough brioche is a project, not a quick bake. But it rewards your patience with bread that's genuinely special — tender, rich, complex, and absolutely worth every moment you put into it.

Make this for a weekend brunch, a holiday morning, or simply because you deserve something beautiful. Once you taste the difference sourdough makes in brioche, you'll never want to make it any other way.
