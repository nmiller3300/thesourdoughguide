# Jalapeño Cheddar Sourdough

This is the loaf that converts people. The moment you cut into it — pockets of melted cheddar, specks of green jalapeño, that open crumb — it's hard to go back to plain sourdough. It's impressive enough to bring to a dinner party and easy enough to make every weekend.

## Ingredients

- **450g** bread flour
- **320g** water, room temperature
- **90g** active sourdough starter (at peak)
- **9g** fine sea salt
- **150g** sharp cheddar cheese, cut into small cubes (not shredded — cubes melt better)
- **2–3** jalapeños, deseeded and diced (keep seeds for more heat)
- **1 tbsp** olive oil (optional, adds richness)

**Yield:** 1 large loaf | **Active time:** 45 minutes | **Total time:** 24–36 hours

---

## Method

### 1. Autolyse (30 minutes)
Mix flour and 300g of the water until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest 30 minutes. This builds gluten without effort.

### 2. Add Starter and Salt
Add starter, remaining water, and salt to the autolysed dough. Squeeze together with your fingers until fully combined. Rest 30 minutes.

### 3. Prepare Add-ins
While the dough rests, dice your jalapeños and cube your cheddar. Pat both dry with a paper towel — excess moisture can make the dough sticky.

### 4. Lamination (fold in fillings)
After the first 30-minute rest, perform a lamination to incorporate the fillings:
1. Wet your hands and gently stretch the dough out on a damp work surface into a large thin rectangle
2. Scatter jalapeños and cheddar evenly over the surface
3. Fold the dough over itself in thirds (like a letter), then rotate 90° and fold again
4. Return to the bowl, cover

### 5. Stretch and Folds
Over the next 2 hours, perform 3 sets of stretch and folds (every 30–40 minutes). Be gentle — you don't want to crush the cheese pockets.

### 6. Bulk Fermentation
After completing stretch and folds, leave covered at room temperature until dough has risen 50–75%, feels airy and jiggly, and shows visible bubbles. **4–8 hours** depending on temperature.

> **Watch carefully:** The cheese adds fat which can slightly slow fermentation. Don't rush it.

### 7. Shape
Turn dough onto a lightly floured surface. Pre-shape into a round, rest 20 minutes. Final shape: build good surface tension and place seam-side up in a well-floured banneton.

### 8. Cold Proof
Cover and refrigerate **10–16 hours**.

### 9. Bake
Preheat oven and Dutch oven to **500°F (260°C)** for 45+ minutes.

Turn cold dough onto parchment. Score with a bold slash. Lower into Dutch oven carefully (some cheese may be near the surface — this is fine, it'll get beautifully caramelized).

- **Covered:** 20 minutes at 500°F
- **Uncovered:** 20–25 minutes at 450°F until deeply golden

**Important:** Cheese near the crust will caramelize and may stick to your Dutch oven. Use parchment paper to prevent this.

### 10. Cool
Cool on a wire rack for at least **1 hour**. The cheese needs time to set. Cut too early and it'll be gooey (not the good kind).

## Tips

**Cheese choice matters.** Extra sharp cheddar has the most flavor. Pepper jack adds extra heat. Gruyère is excellent for a more sophisticated version.

**Heat level.** Deseeded jalapeños are mild — mostly flavor, little heat. Keep the seeds for a significant kick. Pickled jalapeños work too and add a nice tang.

**Cheese placement.** Try to keep cheese away from the very surface during shaping — it can stick to the banneton and tear the crust. A little is fine.

**Variations:** Add caramelized onions, swap cheddar for smoked gouda, or add a tablespoon of garlic powder to the dough for a garlic-cheddar version.

---

This loaf is best eaten the day it's baked, toasted the next day, or made into the greatest grilled cheese you've ever had. Use leftover slices for croutons — they're incredible.
