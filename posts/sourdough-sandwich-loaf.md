# Sourdough Sandwich Loaf

Not every sourdough needs to be a crusty, rustic boule. Sometimes you need a proper sandwich loaf — something with a soft crust, a tight uniform crumb, and slices that hold together under heavy fillings without falling apart.

This is that loaf. It's made in a standard 9×5 inch loaf pan, enriched with butter and a little honey, and produces 12–14 clean slices. It makes the best toast you've ever had.

## Ingredients

- **400g** all-purpose flour (not bread flour — all-purpose gives a softer, more tender crumb)
- **200g** warm milk (about 100°F — warmer than room temp but not hot)
- **80g** water, room temperature
- **100g** active sourdough starter
- **25g** unsalted butter, softened
- **20g** honey
- **8g** fine sea salt

**For the top:**
- 1 tbsp softened butter (for brushing after baking)
- Optional: rolled oats, sesame seeds, or flaky salt

**Yield:** 1 standard loaf (9×5 inch pan) | **Total time:** 18–28 hours

---

## Method

### 1. Mix (Evening, Day 1)
1. Combine milk, water, starter, and honey. Stir well.
2. Add flour and salt. Mix until a cohesive dough forms.
3. Add softened butter, a little at a time, squeezing it into the dough until fully incorporated.
4. The dough should feel soft, slightly tacky, and smooth — more like a brioche dough than rustic sourdough.

### 2. Knead or Mix
Knead by hand for **6–8 minutes** until the dough is smooth and passes the windowpane test (stretch a small piece — it should stretch thin enough to see light through without tearing). Alternatively, mix in a stand mixer with a dough hook for 5 minutes.

### 3. Bulk Fermentation (Room Temperature)
Cover and leave at room temperature **4–8 hours** until dough has risen 50–75%. The honey and milk will slow fermentation slightly — don't rush.

### 4. Refrigerate Overnight
Cover tightly and refrigerate **10–16 hours**. This cold ferment develops flavor and makes shaping easier.

---

### Day 2

### 5. Shape for the Pan
1. Remove dough from fridge. It will be firm and very easy to handle.
2. Turn onto a lightly floured surface. Flatten into a rough rectangle about as wide as your loaf pan is long.
3. Roll the rectangle tightly toward you (like rolling up a towel), creating a log shape.
4. Pinch the seam closed along the bottom.
5. Place seam-side down in a **greased 9×5 inch loaf pan**.

### 6. Final Proof
Cover loosely with plastic wrap or a damp kitchen towel. Let rise at room temperature until the dough crowns **1–1.5 inches above the rim** of the pan — this can take **3–6 hours** from cold, depending on temperature.

**Do not skip this step or rush it.** The crown above the pan is what gives you a beautiful domed loaf. Undercooled means a flat top; overproofed means it collapses in the oven.

### 7. Bake
Preheat oven to **375°F (190°C)**.

Optionally, brush the top with milk or egg wash for a golden sheen, and sprinkle with oats or sesame seeds.

Bake **35–40 minutes** until deep golden brown on top and the internal temperature reads **195–200°F (90–93°C)** on an instant-read thermometer. The thermometer is the best way to know it's done.

**If the top is browning too fast:** Tent loosely with foil at the 25-minute mark.

### 8. Cool
Immediately brush the hot top with the tablespoon of softened butter — it gives the crust a soft, glossy finish.

Turn out of the pan and cool on a wire rack for **at least 1.5 hours** before slicing. Sandwich bread especially needs this time for the crumb to fully set.

---

## Slicing Tips

A good serrated bread knife makes all the difference. Slice with a gentle sawing motion — pressing down tears the crumb.

For uniform slices, mark the loaf with a ruler and toothpicks before cutting. Aim for ¾ inch thickness for sandwiches, ½ inch for toast.

---

## Variations

**Whole wheat sandwich loaf:** Replace 100g all-purpose flour with whole wheat flour. Increase water by 15g.

**Milk bread (ultra-soft):** Replace all the water with whole milk. Add an extra 15g butter. This produces an exceptionally tender, pillowy loaf (think Japanese milk bread texture with sourdough flavor).

**Seeded:** Add 40g of mixed seeds (sesame, sunflower, poppy) to the dough during mixing.

**Cinnamon raisin:** Add 1 tsp cinnamon to the flour and fold in 100g plump raisins (soaked in warm water first) during mixing. Roll with a cinnamon-sugar filling before shaping.

---

## Storage

This loaf keeps well at room temperature, wrapped in a kitchen towel or bread bag, for **4–5 days**. The enriched dough retains moisture longer than lean sourdough.

Freezes well — slice first, freeze in a zip-lock, toast from frozen. Makes exceptional French toast on day 3 or 4 when it's slightly drier.
