There's something almost magical about naan — those charred bubbles, that impossibly soft interior, the way it tears into perfect pieces for scooping up curry. And when you add sourdough to the equation? You get all of that plus a subtle tanginess that makes every bite more interesting.

This sourdough naan is the real deal. It's tender from yogurt, enriched with a touch of butter, and fermented overnight for flavor that store-bought flatbreads simply can't match. The best part? You don't need a tandoor oven. A screaming hot cast iron skillet does the job beautifully.

## Why Sourdough Makes Better Naan

Traditional naan relies on commercial yeast for its rise. It works, but it's one-dimensional. Sourdough brings complexity — a gentle tang that plays beautifully against rich, creamy curries. The overnight fermentation also breaks down the flour's starches and proteins, making the bread more digestible and giving it that coveted soft, almost stretchy texture.

Using active starter (rather than discard) ensures you get enough rise to create those signature bubbles that char so beautifully in the pan.

## Yield

**8 medium naan** (approximately 7 inches each)

## Ingredients

### For the Dough

| Ingredient | Grams | Volume |
|------------|-------|--------|
| Bread flour | 400g | 3¼ cups |
| Active sourdough starter (fed 4-8 hours prior) | 100g | ½ cup |
| Full-fat plain yogurt | 120g | ½ cup |
| Warm water (90-95°F / 32-35°C) | 100g | scant ½ cup |
| Melted butter (or ghee) | 30g | 2 tablespoons |
| Honey or sugar | 15g | 1 tablespoon |
| Fine sea salt | 8g | 1½ teaspoons |

### For Cooking and Finishing

| Ingredient | Amount |
|------------|--------|
| Butter or ghee (for brushing) | 60g / 4 tablespoons |
| Garlic, minced (optional) | 3 cloves |
| Fresh cilantro, chopped (optional) | 2 tablespoons |
| Flaky sea salt | to taste |

## Equipment Needed

- Large mixing bowl
- Kitchen scale
- Cast iron skillet (10-12 inch) or griddle
- Rolling pin
- Clean kitchen towels
- Pastry brush

## Method

### Day One: Mix and Ferment

**1. Combine wet ingredients (5 minutes)**

In a large bowl, whisk together the active sourdough starter, yogurt, warm water, melted butter, and honey until smooth. The mixture should look slightly creamy and smell pleasantly tangy.

**2. Add flour and salt (5 minutes)**

Add the bread flour and salt to the wet ingredients. Use a wooden spoon or your hand to mix until a shaggy dough forms. Don't worry about smoothness yet — you're just hydrating the flour.

**3. Knead the dough (8-10 minutes)**

Turn the dough onto a clean, unfloured surface. Knead by pushing the dough away with the heel of your hand, folding it back over itself, and rotating a quarter turn. Repeat until the dough is smooth, soft, and slightly tacky but not sticky. It should spring back gently when poked.

If the dough feels too dry, wet your hands slightly while kneading. If it's too sticky, resist adding more flour — it will come together with continued kneading.

**4. First rise — bulk fermentation (8-12 hours)**

Shape the dough into a ball and place it in a lightly oiled bowl. Cover with a damp towel or plastic wrap. Leave at room temperature (70-75°F / 21-24°C) for 8-12 hours, or overnight.

The dough should roughly double in size and look puffy with a few bubbles on the surface. In cooler kitchens, this may take closer to 14 hours.

### Day Two: Shape and Cook

**5. Divide the dough (5 minutes)**

Gently turn the dough onto a lightly floured surface. Try not to deflate it too aggressively — you want to preserve some of those fermentation bubbles. Divide into 8 equal pieces (approximately 95g each).

**6. Pre-shape into balls (3 minutes)**

Shape each piece into a tight ball by cupping your hand over the dough and moving it in small circles on the counter. The surface tension helps the naan hold its shape. Cover the balls with a damp towel and let rest for 20-30 minutes. This relaxation makes rolling much easier.

**7. Prepare your garlic butter (2 minutes)**

While the dough rests, melt the butter in a small saucepan over low heat. If using garlic, add the minced cloves and cook for 30 seconds until fragrant but not browned. Remove from heat and stir in the chopped cilantro if using. Set aside.

**8. Heat your skillet (5 minutes)**

Place a cast iron skillet over high heat. Let it get screaming hot — this is essential for those charred bubbles. After 5 minutes, the pan should be nearly smoking. Reduce heat to medium-high.

**9. Roll the naan (ongoing)**

Working one at a time, roll a dough ball into an oval or teardrop shape, about 7 inches long and ¼ inch thick. Don't stress about perfection — rustic shapes are traditional and beautiful. Lightly flour your surface only if needed.

**10. Cook the naan (2-3 minutes per naan)**

Lay the rolled naan directly onto the dry, hot skillet. Within 30-60 seconds, you'll see bubbles forming on the surface. When the bottom develops golden-brown char spots (about 1-2 minutes), flip using tongs.

Cook the second side for another 60-90 seconds until charred and cooked through. The naan should be pliable, not crispy.

**11. Brush and serve immediately**

Transfer the hot naan to a plate and immediately brush generously with the garlic butter. Sprinkle with flaky sea salt. Stack cooked naan under a clean towel to keep warm while you cook the rest.

Repeat with remaining dough balls, adjusting heat if the pan gets too hot or too cool.

## Baker's Tips

**The hotter the better.** Naan needs intense heat to puff and char properly. If your naan is cooking through but not getting those beautiful spots, your pan isn't hot enough. Let it heat longer between batches if needed.

**Use full-fat yogurt.** Low-fat or non-fat yogurt will make the naan less tender. The fat contributes to that signature soft, almost silky texture.

**Don't over-roll.** Keeping the naan about ¼ inch thick allows steam to build inside, creating those pockets and bubbles. Paper-thin naan won't puff properly.

## Storage Instructions

Naan is best eaten fresh and warm, but leftovers keep surprisingly well:

**Room temperature:** Stack cooled naan in a zip-lock bag or wrap in foil. Keeps 1-2 days.

**Refrigerator:** Store in an airtight container for up to 5 days.

**Freezer:** Wrap individual naan in plastic wrap, then place in a freezer bag. Freeze for up to 2 months.

**To reheat:** Warm in a dry skillet over medium heat for 30 seconds per side, or wrap in damp paper towel and microwave for 15-20 seconds. For frozen naan, thaw at room temperature before reheating.

## Variations

### Keema Naan (Stuffed with Spiced Meat)

Brown 150g ground lamb or beef with minced onion, ginger, garlic, cumin, and garam masala. Cool completely. Place a tablespoon of filling in the center of each rolled naan, fold edges over, and re-roll gently to seal. Cook as directed.

### Peshawari Naan (Sweet Coconut Filling)

Mix 50g desiccated coconut, 30g ground almonds, 2 tablespoons sugar, and 1 tablespoon melted butter. Add a spoonful to each naan before final rolling. These are incredible with rich, creamy kormas.

### Herb and Nigella Seed Naan

Press nigella seeds (kalonji) and fresh cilantro leaves into the surface of rolled naan before cooking. The seeds toast in the hot pan and add wonderful earthy, onion-like notes.

### Whole Wheat Naan

Substitute 100g of the bread flour with whole wheat flour. The naan will be slightly denser but more nutritious. You may need an additional tablespoon of water to achieve the right dough consistency.
