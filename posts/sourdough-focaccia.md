# Sourdough Focaccia

Focaccia is the perfect entry point into high-hydration sourdough baking. It's forgiving, it's fast (relatively), and the results are spectacular — a thick, olive-oil-soaked slab with a shattering crust and a pillowy, open interior.

No shaping skills required. No banneton. No Dutch oven. Just a 9×13 baking pan and a lot of good olive oil.

## Ingredients

**For the dough:**
- **500g** bread flour
- **425g** water, room temperature (85% hydration)
- **100g** active sourdough starter
- **10g** fine sea salt
- **30g** good olive oil (plus more for the pan and topping)

**For the topping:**
- **60–80ml** high-quality olive oil (don't skimp — this is the whole point)
- **1 tsp** flaky sea salt (Maldon or fleur de sel)
- Fresh rosemary sprigs
- **Optional:** sliced olives, cherry tomatoes, thinly sliced onion, roasted garlic

---

## Method

### Day 1 — Mix and Bulk

**Morning or midday:**

1. Combine flour, water, starter, and salt in a large bowl. Mix until no dry flour remains. The dough will be very wet — this is correct for focaccia.

2. Add 30g olive oil. Mix in with your hands, squeezing it through the dough until absorbed.

3. Cover and rest 30 minutes.

4. Perform **4 sets of stretch and folds** over 2 hours (every 30 minutes). The dough will go from shaggy to smooth and airy.

5. **Bulk ferment** at room temperature until nearly doubled — **6–10 hours** at 72°F. It should look bubbly and puffy.

---

### Day 1 Evening — Pan and Refrigerate

1. Pour **3 tbsp olive oil** into a 9×13 inch (23×33 cm) baking pan and tilt to coat.

2. Gently transfer the dough into the oiled pan. It will be loose — don't fight it.

3. Fold the dough over itself once or twice (like folding a letter), then flip it so the smooth side faces up.

4. Drizzle another tablespoon of olive oil over the top.

5. Cover tightly with plastic wrap and **refrigerate overnight** (8–16 hours).

---

### Day 2 — Dimple, Top, and Bake

1. Remove the pan from the fridge and let it come to room temperature — **2–3 hours**. The dough should spread to fill the pan and look very puffy.

2. **Preheat oven to 450°F (230°C)**. Place a rack in the lower third.

3. Drizzle remaining olive oil generously over the surface.

4. Oil your fingers and dimple the dough aggressively — press all the way to the bottom of the pan. Don't be shy. Those dimples collect oil and make the texture.

5. Scatter rosemary sprigs, toppings of your choice, and flaky salt over the surface.

6. **Bake 25–30 minutes** until deep golden brown on top and the edges are crispy and pulling away from the sides.

7. Immediately run a spatula under the focaccia and transfer to a wire rack. Don't let it sit in the pan — it'll steam and lose its crust.

---

## Topping Combinations

**Classic:** Rosemary + flaky salt + olive oil  
**Tomato & olive:** Halved cherry tomatoes + sliced kalamata olives + oregano  
**Onion & herb:** Caramelized onions + fresh thyme + sea salt  
**Garlic:** Roasted garlic cloves pressed into the dimples + rosemary  
**Fig & brie:** Fresh or dried figs + small brie pieces (bake uncovered from the start)

---

## Serving

Focaccia is best eaten the day it's baked — ideally within a few hours. It's incredible as:
- A sandwich base (slice horizontally)
- An appetizer with olive oil for dipping
- A side to pasta or soup
- A base for a focaccia pizza

Leftovers reheat beautifully at 350°F for 8–10 minutes. Don't microwave it — you'll lose everything that makes it good.

---

The secret to great focaccia is olive oil and confidence. Use more oil than feels reasonable, dimple deeper than feels right, and bake longer than feels safe. You want dark gold, not pale yellow.
