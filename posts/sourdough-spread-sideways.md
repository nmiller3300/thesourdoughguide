# Why Did My Sourdough Spread Sideways?

You shaped a beautiful round boule, placed it in the banneton, and slid it into the oven. But instead of rising dramatically into a tall, domed loaf, it spread flat and wide — more pancake than bread.

This is a gluten or proofing problem. Here's how to figure out which one.

## The Two Causes of a Spreading Loaf

**Cause 1: Over-fermented/over-proofed dough.** When dough ferments too long, the gluten that holds the loaf's structure gets degraded by acids produced during fermentation. The gluten can no longer support the weight of the loaf, so it collapses outward instead of upward.

**Cause 2: Insufficient gluten strength.** Even properly fermented dough can spread if gluten wasn't developed enough to hold the structure. This often comes from skipping stretch and folds, high hydration without proper development, or inadequate surface tension during shaping.

---

## How to Tell Which One You Have

**It's over-fermentation if:**
- The dough felt very slack and fragile when you scored it
- The poke test showed no spring-back at all (finger indent stayed)
- The shaped loaf started spreading even in the banneton before baking
- The bread has good flavor but flat structure

**It's insufficient gluten if:**
- The dough felt fine going in but spread during baking
- You skipped some or all stretch and fold sets
- The loaf spread evenly in all directions rather than just flopping
- The crumb has decent structure but the shape is flat

---

## Fixing Over-Fermentation

**Shorten bulk fermentation.** This is the core fix. In warm kitchens, bulk ferment can complete 30–60% faster than recipe timing suggests. Watch the dough, not the clock. Target 50–75% volume increase — not doubling.

**The poke test is your best friend.** Before shaping, poke the dough about ½ inch deep with a floured finger. It should spring back slowly to about halfway. If it springs back completely: needs more time. If it doesn't spring back at all: overproofed — shape and bake immediately.

**In warm kitchens, check dough every hour** during the final stages of bulk fermentation.

---

## Fixing Insufficient Gluten Development

**Do all your stretch and fold sets.** Four sets spaced 30 minutes apart during bulk fermentation builds the gluten network that holds the loaf's shape. This step isn't optional for high-hydration doughs.

**Build surface tension when shaping.** After pre-shaping, rest the dough 20 minutes. During final shaping, drag the boule on an unfloured counter with cupped hands until you feel it tighten. A properly tensioned loaf holds its shape going into the banneton and coming out.

**Bake cold from the fridge.** Cold dough holds its shape much better in the critical first minutes of baking before the crust sets. A loaf baked straight from the fridge spreads far less than one baked at room temperature.

---

## The Fastest Single Fix

If you've been baking your dough at room temperature after proofing, start baking it cold instead. Shape the dough, put it in the banneton, cover, and refrigerate overnight. Bake it straight from the fridge into the screaming hot Dutch oven.

This one change alone solves the spreading problem for most bakers. Cold dough scores cleanly, holds its shape, and produces better oven spring.
