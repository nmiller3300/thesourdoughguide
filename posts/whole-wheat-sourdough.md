# Whole Wheat Sourdough

This is the loaf for people who want more than just white sourdough. Whole wheat flour adds a nutty, complex depth that plain bread flour can't match — and the extra fiber makes it genuinely more nutritious without tasting like health food.

The trick is the ratio. 100% whole wheat sourdough is dense and challenging. 30% whole wheat hits the sweet spot: all the flavor benefits with none of the structural compromises.

## Ingredients

- **315g** bread flour (70%)
- **135g** whole wheat flour (30%)
- **340g** water (75% hydration — slightly higher than white to compensate for thirstier whole wheat)
- **90g** active sourdough starter
- **9g** fine sea salt

**Yield:** 1 loaf | **Total time:** 24–36 hours

---

## Why Whole Wheat Needs More Water

Whole wheat flour contains the bran from the grain, which absorbs significantly more water than white flour. If you use the same hydration as a white loaf, you'll get a stiffer, drier dough. The extra water in this recipe keeps it workable and produces a more open crumb.

**Bran also cuts gluten strands** — the sharp edges of bran particles literally slice through gluten networks as they develop. This is why whole wheat loaves tend toward a tighter crumb. Going to 30% instead of 50% or 100% minimizes this effect while preserving flavor.

---

## Method

### 1. Autolyse — 45 Minutes
Mix both flours and 320g of the water until no dry flour remains. The dough may feel stiffer than a pure white dough — that's the bran absorbing moisture. Cover and rest **45 minutes** (longer than a white loaf autolyse for the same reason).

### 2. Add Starter and Salt
Add starter and remaining 20g water. Pinch and fold to incorporate. Add salt, incorporate again. The dough should feel slightly tacky but workable.

### 3. Stretch and Folds — 2.5 Hours
Perform **5 sets of stretch and folds**, spaced 30 minutes apart. Whole wheat dough develops strength faster than white, so you'll notice it getting tighter and more elastic by set 3 or 4.

### 4. Bulk Fermentation
Leave covered at room temperature until dough has increased 50–65% in volume. **4–7 hours** at 72–75°F. Whole wheat ferments slightly faster than white flour due to higher enzyme activity — keep an eye on it.

Signs it's ready:
- Domed top (not flat)
- Bubbles visible through the bowl
- Dough jiggles when you shake the container
- Webby, airy texture when you pull a small piece

### 5. Shape
Shape exactly as you would a white sourdough loaf. The dough may feel slightly less extensible — that's normal. Be confident and build good surface tension without overworking it.

Place seam-side up in a well-floured banneton. Whole wheat dough sticks more — be generous with flour (rice flour works better than bread flour for this).

### 6. Cold Proof
Refrigerate **8–14 hours**. The cold proof is especially important for whole wheat — it develops flavor complexity that compensates for the earthier, less neutral taste of the flour.

### 7. Bake
**500°F preheated Dutch oven.**

Score with confidence — whole wheat dough is slightly denser and the ear won't spring as dramatically as white, but it will still open beautifully.

- **Covered:** 20 minutes
- **Uncovered:** 20–25 minutes until deep brown

Whole wheat loaves take color faster because of the natural sugars in the bran. If it's browning quickly, reduce to 430°F for the uncovered phase.

### 8. Cool
At least **1.5 hours** before cutting. Whole wheat bread needs more time for the crumb to set than white.

## Variations

**50% Whole Wheat:** Increase whole wheat to 225g, decrease bread flour to 225g. Increase water to 360g. Expect a slightly tighter crumb but deeper flavor.

**Add seeds:** Fold in 40g of sesame, sunflower, or pumpkin seeds during lamination. Toast them first for better flavor.

**Honey whole wheat:** Add 20g honey with the starter. It feeds the yeast, adds subtle sweetness, and improves browning.

---

## Storing

Whole wheat sourdough actually keeps better than white — the bran retains moisture. Store cut-side down on a wooden board for up to 4 days. Freezes well: slice first, freeze in a bag, toast from frozen.

The flavor also improves overnight. Day 2 whole wheat sourdough is often better than day 1.
