# How to Start a Sourdough Starter From Scratch

Building a sourdough starter from scratch is easier than most people think — and it's the most satisfying part of the whole sourdough journey. You're essentially cultivating a living culture of wild yeast and bacteria that will leaven your bread for years to come.

Here's exactly how to do it, day by day.

## What You Need

- **Flour** — All-purpose or whole wheat (whole wheat gets things going faster)
- **Water** — Filtered or left out overnight to dechlorinate
- **A clean jar** — At least 1 quart capacity
- **A kitchen scale** — Essential for accuracy
- **Time** — 7–14 days of daily feeding

## The Basic Process

Every day, you'll do the same thing: **discard** half your starter, then **feed** it fresh flour and water. This keeps it from getting too acidic and gives the wild yeast fresh food to eat.

**Ratio:** 1:1:1 — equal parts starter, flour, and water by weight.

---

## Day-by-Day Guide

### Day 1 — The Beginning

Mix together in a clean jar:
- 50g flour
- 50g room temperature water

Stir vigorously until no dry flour remains. Cover loosely (not airtight — it needs to breathe) and leave at room temperature, ideally 70–78°F.

### Day 2 — First Signs

You might see a bubble or two — or nothing yet. Both are normal. Either way, discard half your starter (down to about 50g) and feed with 50g flour + 50g water. Stir well and cover.

> **Don't be discouraged** if Day 2 looks dead. The wild yeast takes time to establish.

### Days 3–4 — Activity Picks Up

By now you should see more bubbles and a slight rise. The smell will be strong — almost like cheese or beer. That's lactic acid forming. Keep discarding and feeding daily.

### Days 5–6 — It's Alive

Your starter should be doubling in size between feedings and smelling more pleasantly sour. At this point, start the **float test**: drop a small spoonful into water. If it floats, there's enough gas for baking.

### Day 7 — Ready to Bake

If your starter consistently:
- Doubles within 4–8 hours of feeding
- Has lots of bubbles throughout
- Passes the float test
- Smells pleasantly tangy

**You're ready to bake your first loaf.**

---

## Common Problems

**Still not active by Day 7?**
- Move it somewhere warmer
- Try whole wheat flour instead of all-purpose
- Make sure your water isn't chlorinated

**Too much liquid on top (hooch)?**
This is normal — it's alcohol produced by the yeast. Stir it in or pour it off, then feed as usual.

**Smells terrible?**
Slightly funky is normal, especially early on. Pink or orange mold, or a truly putrid smell = start over with a clean jar.

---

## Maintaining Your Starter Long-Term

Once established, your starter can live in the fridge and be fed once a week. Pull it out the night before you want to bake, feed it, and let it peak at room temperature before using.

A well-maintained starter lasts indefinitely — some bakers use starters that are decades old.
