# Classic Country Sourdough Loaf

This is the recipe you'll make every week once you get it right. No fancy equipment, no obscure techniques — just flour, water, salt, and a good starter producing a deeply satisfying loaf with a crackly crust and open, chewy crumb.

## Ingredients

- **450g** bread flour (or all-purpose)
- **325g** water, room temperature (filtered or left out overnight)
- **90g** active sourdough starter (at peak — doubled and bubbly)
- **9g** fine sea salt

**Yield:** 1 loaf | **Total time:** 24–36 hours (mostly hands-off)

---

## Equipment

- Kitchen scale (essential — no volume measurements)
- Large mixing bowl
- Bench scraper
- Banneton or bowl lined with floured kitchen towel
- Dutch oven (5–6 quart)
- Lame or sharp razor blade

---

## Step-by-Step Method

### 1. Mix the Dough (5 minutes)

In a large bowl, combine the flour and 300g of the water (hold back 25g). Mix until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest for **30 minutes** — this is the autolyse, and it develops gluten without effort.

After 30 minutes, add your starter and the remaining 25g water. Squeeze the starter through the dough with your fingers until fully incorporated. Add the salt and mix again.

### 2. Stretch and Fold (2 hours)

Over the next 2 hours, perform **4 sets of stretch and folds**, spaced 30 minutes apart.

For each set: grab the dough from one side, stretch it up and fold it over to the opposite side. Rotate the bowl 90° and repeat. Do this 4 times per set.

After all 4 sets, your dough should feel smoother, stronger, and slightly elastic.

### 3. Bulk Fermentation (4–8 hours)

Leave the dough covered at room temperature to ferment. This is the most variable step — it depends entirely on your kitchen temperature.

**Signs the bulk is done:**
- Dough has increased 50–75% in volume
- Surface looks domed and slightly bubbly
- Dough feels lighter and jiggly (not dense)
- When you pull a piece, you can see air bubbles inside

> **Warm kitchen (75–78°F):** 4–5 hours
> **Cool kitchen (68–72°F):** 6–8 hours

### 4. Shape

Lightly flour your work surface. Gently turn the dough out — don't deflate it.

Pre-shape into a rough round by pulling the edges toward the center. Let it rest uncovered for 20–30 minutes.

For final shaping: flip the dough over, stretch the bottom edge toward you and fold up, then fold in the sides, then roll the dough toward you, building tension on the surface. Place seam-side up into your floured banneton.

### 5. Cold Proof (8–16 hours)

Cover loosely with plastic wrap or a shower cap and refrigerate overnight. This slow cold proof develops flavor and makes the dough easier to score.

You can bake it anywhere from 8 to 16 hours later — it's forgiving.

### 6. Bake

**Preheat** your oven to **500°F (260°C)** with your Dutch oven inside for at least **45 minutes**. This is non-negotiable — the Dutch oven needs to be screaming hot.

When ready to bake:
1. Cut a piece of parchment paper
2. Turn your dough out of the banneton onto the parchment
3. Score with a lame — one confident slash at a 30–45° angle, about ½ inch deep
4. Lower the dough (on the parchment) into the Dutch oven
5. Cover and bake **20 minutes**
6. Remove lid, reduce to **450°F**, bake **20–25 more minutes** until deep golden brown
7. Cool on a wire rack for **at least 1 hour** before cutting

## Reading Your Loaf

**Good signs:**
- Deep golden to dark brown crust
- Hollow sound when tapped on the bottom
- "Ear" raised from the score
- Open crumb when sliced

**Common issues:**
- Flat with no ear → over-proofed or weak starter
- Dense gummy crumb → under-fermented
- Pale crust → oven not hot enough or lid left on too long
- Huge holes but gummy → too much water for your skill level yet

---

## Storage

Sourdough keeps well at room temperature, cut-side down on a wooden board, for 3–4 days. Don't refrigerate — it stales faster. Freeze slices wrapped in plastic for up to 3 months.

The first time you pull this from the oven and hear it crackling as it cools, you'll understand why people become obsessed with sourdough. That sound is the crust contracting — your reward for 24 hours of patience. Make it again next week. It gets better every time.
