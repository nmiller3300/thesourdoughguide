There's a moment when you lift the lid of your waffle iron and see what's waiting inside — golden, deeply cratered, impossibly crisp — that makes every early morning worth it. Now imagine that waffle has a subtle tang, a more complex flavor, and a texture that somehow manages to be both shatteringly crispy and cloud-soft at the same time.

That's a sourdough waffle. And once you make them, you'll never go back.

## Why Sourdough Waffles Are Different

The magic here isn't just about using up discard (though that's a beautiful bonus). The fermentation that happens when you mix your batter the night before does things that regular waffle batter simply can't achieve.

First, there's flavor. That gentle tang from the sourdough balances beautifully against maple syrup, fresh fruit, or even savory toppings. It's not sour-sour — it's nuanced, interesting, the kind of flavor that makes people ask what your secret is.

Then there's texture. The overnight fermentation breaks down some of the starches and develops the proteins in ways that create an exceptionally tender interior while allowing the outside to crisp up beautifully. The contrast is remarkable.

Finally, there's digestibility. Many people who struggle with regular wheat products find that long-fermented sourdough treats them more kindly. The fermentation process begins breaking down some of the compounds that can cause digestive discomfort.

## Yield

Makes approximately 6-8 Belgian-style waffles or 10-12 standard waffles, depending on your iron.

## Ingredients

### For the Overnight Batter

| Ingredient | Grams | Volume |
|------------|-------|--------|
| All-purpose flour | 250g | 2 cups |
| Sourdough discard (unfed starter) | 150g | about ⅔ cup |
| Whole milk | 360g | 1½ cups |
| Granulated sugar | 25g | 2 tablespoons |
| Fine sea salt | 6g | 1 teaspoon |

### Added in the Morning

| Ingredient | Grams | Volume |
|------------|-------|--------|
| Unsalted butter, melted and slightly cooled | 85g | 6 tablespoons |
| Large eggs | 2 | 2 |
| Baking soda | 4g | ¾ teaspoon |
| Vanilla extract | 5g | 1 teaspoon |

## Equipment Needed

- Large mixing bowl
- Whisk
- Waffle iron (Belgian or standard)
- Small bowl for eggs
- Rubber spatula

## Method

### The Night Before (5 minutes active time)

1. **Combine the overnight ingredients.** In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the flour, sourdough discard, milk, sugar, and salt until no dry pockets remain. The batter will be thick and slightly lumpy — that's perfect. Don't overmix.

2. **Cover and rest.** Place plastic wrap directly on the surface of the batter or cover the bowl tightly. Leave at room temperature overnight, or for at least 8 hours and up to 12 hours.

### In the Morning (10 minutes active time)

3. **Observe your batter.** When you uncover it, you should see bubbles throughout and notice that the batter has expanded slightly. It will smell pleasantly tangy. This is exactly what you want — the fermentation has done its work.

4. **Preheat your waffle iron.** Turn it on to medium-high heat. If your iron has numbered settings, start around 4 or 5. You can adjust after your first waffle.

5. **Prepare the morning additions.** In a small bowl, lightly beat the eggs. Melt the butter and let it cool for a minute or two — you don't want it hot enough to scramble the eggs.

6. **Finish the batter.** Add the beaten eggs, melted butter, baking soda, and vanilla to the overnight batter. Whisk gently until just combined. The batter will immediately become lighter and fluffier as the baking soda reacts with the acidic sourdough. You'll see it puff up slightly. Work quickly from this point — that reaction is what gives you lift.

7. **Check your iron.** If you have a nonstick waffle iron in good condition, you likely won't need to grease it. If your waffles tend to stick, brush lightly with melted butter or use a neutral cooking spray.

8. **Cook the waffles.** Pour batter onto the center of your hot waffle iron — about ⅔ to ¾ cup for Belgian irons, about ½ cup for standard irons. Close the lid and cook until steam stops escaping and the waffle is deeply golden, about 4-5 minutes for Belgian waffles, 3-4 minutes for standard.

9. **Check for doneness.** The waffle should release easily and be golden brown across its entire surface. If it's pale, give it another minute. If it's sticking, it needs more time.

10. **Serve immediately or keep warm.** For the crispiest waffles, serve directly from the iron. If cooking for a crowd, place finished waffles in a single layer on a wire rack set in a 200°F (93°C) oven. Never stack them — trapped steam is the enemy of crispy waffles.

## Tips for Perfect Sourdough Waffles

**Don't skip the overnight fermentation.** I know it's tempting to just throw everything together in the morning, but the overnight rest is where the magic happens. The flavor development and texture improvement are worth the minimal planning required.

**Use discard straight from the fridge.** Cold, unfed discard works beautifully here. You don't need to feed your starter first — in fact, hungry, acidic discard gives you better tang and better rise when it meets the baking soda.

**The baking soda timing matters.** Once you add the baking soda, the clock is ticking. That chemical reaction creates the gas that makes your waffles light and airy. Mix it in, and start cooking within a few minutes.

**Listen to your waffle iron.** Most irons stop steaming when the waffle is done. Open it too early and you'll tear the waffle; leave it too long and you'll have something closer to a cracker. Pay attention to what your specific iron tells you.

## Storage Instructions

**Leftover batter:** The fully mixed batter (with eggs, butter, and baking soda added) doesn't store well — the baking soda loses its power. However, the overnight batter without the morning additions can stay in the refrigerator for up to 3 days. Just add the morning ingredients when you're ready to cook.

**Cooked waffles:** Let waffles cool completely on a wire rack, then freeze in a single layer on a baking sheet. Once frozen solid, transfer to a freezer bag or airtight container. They'll keep for up to 2 months.

**Reheating:** Toast frozen waffles directly from the freezer in a toaster or toaster oven at medium heat. This actually re-crisps them beautifully — often better than trying to rewarm fresh waffles. A regular oven at 350°F (175°C) works too; about 5-7 minutes directly on the rack.

## Variations

### Whole Wheat Sourdough Waffles
Replace half the all-purpose flour (125g) with whole wheat flour. The waffles will be slightly denser and nuttier, with a more rustic character. Increase the milk by about 2 tablespoons (30g) to compensate for the extra absorption.

### Buttermilk Sourdough Waffles
Replace the whole milk with buttermilk for extra tang and tenderness. The additional acidity means even more reaction with the baking soda, so these waffles come out particularly light.

### Savory Herb Waffles
Omit the sugar and vanilla. Add 2 tablespoons of finely chopped fresh herbs (chives, thyme, and rosemary work beautifully) and ½ cup (50g) of finely grated Parmesan to the morning additions. These are incredible topped with a fried egg and some sautéed greens.

### Chocolate Chip Waffles
Fold ¾ cup (130g) of chocolate chips into the finished batter just before cooking. The chips will melt into gooey pockets throughout the waffle. Kids go absolutely wild for these.

### Cinnamon Roll Waffles
Add 1½ teaspoons of ground cinnamon to the overnight batter. Drizzle finished waffles with a simple glaze made from 1 cup powdered sugar mixed with 2-3 tablespoons of milk and ½ teaspoon vanilla.

## Final Thoughts

Sourdough waffles represent everything I love about baking with a starter. They take something ordinary — weekend waffles — and elevate it into something memorable. The overnight fermentation isn't fussy; it's actually easier than mixing batter from scratch in the morning when you're barely awake.

And there's something deeply satisfying about knowing that the jar of discard in your fridge, the one that might otherwise get tossed, transforms into breakfast that makes your whole family gather around the table.

Start the batter tonight. Tomorrow morning, you'll thank yourself.
