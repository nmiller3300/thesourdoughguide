# Sourdough Discard Chocolate Chip Cookies

This is what happens when sourdough meets the best cookie recipe of all time. The discard adds a barely perceptible tang — you won't taste sourdough, but you'll taste something more complex, more interesting, more *why can't I stop eating these* than any standard cookie.

The lactic acids in the discard react with the sugar and butter to create a slightly toffee-like undertone. The texture is chewier and more substantial. And because you're using discard, you're reducing waste while making something genuinely exceptional.

## Ingredients

*(Makes 20–24 cookies)*

- **225g** (2 sticks) unsalted butter
- **200g** (1 cup packed) brown sugar, dark preferred
- **100g** (½ cup) granulated white sugar
- **150g** sourdough discard (unfed, straight from the fridge is fine)
- **2** large eggs, room temperature
- **2 tsp** vanilla extract
- **280g** (2¼ cups) all-purpose flour
- **1 tsp** baking soda
- **1 tsp** fine sea salt
- **350g** (12 oz) chocolate chips or chopped chocolate (use good chocolate)

**Optional but recommended:**
- 1 tsp espresso powder (amplifies chocolate flavor)
- Flaky salt for finishing (Maldon or fleur de sel)

---

## The Technique That Makes These Perfect

**Brown your butter.** This is optional but produces a cookie 40% better than standard creamed butter. Place butter in a light-colored saucepan over medium heat. It will foam, then subside, then foam again — at this second foam, small brown bits will appear at the bottom and the butter will smell nutty and toasty. Immediately pour into your mixing bowl and let cool to room temperature. The water has cooked off, concentrating the milk solids, and the Maillard reaction has happened. Your cookies will thank you.

---

## Method

### 1. Cream
Beat cooled brown butter (or softened butter) with both sugars until well combined and slightly fluffy — **2–3 minutes** with a hand mixer or stand mixer.

### 2. Add Wet Ingredients
Add eggs one at a time, mixing after each. Add vanilla and sourdough discard. Mix until smooth and homogenous. The discard may look curdled at first — keep mixing.

### 3. Add Dry Ingredients
Whisk flour, baking soda, salt, and espresso powder (if using) together. Add to the wet mixture and mix until just combined — **don't overmix**. Overmixing develops gluten and makes cookies tough.

### 4. Fold in Chocolate
Fold in chocolate chips or chopped chocolate by hand with a spatula.

### 5. Chill the Dough — This Step Matters
Cover and refrigerate **at least 1 hour**, or up to **72 hours**. 

Cold dough spreads less, meaning thicker cookies. The resting time also allows the flavors to meld and intensifies the sourdough notes. 48–72 hours in the fridge produces noticeably more complex, nuanced cookies. It's worth the wait.

### 6. Bake
Preheat oven to **375°F (190°C)**.

Line baking sheets with parchment paper.

Scoop cold dough into balls using a **2-tablespoon cookie scoop** (or roll into balls about 1.5 inches in diameter). Space **3 inches apart** — these spread.

**Sprinkle generously with flaky salt** before baking.

Bake **10–12 minutes** until the edges are set and golden but the center still looks slightly underdone. This is correct — they'll firm up as they cool. Overbaked cookies lose everything that makes them special.

**Let cool on the pan 5 minutes** before transferring to a wire rack. They'll crumble if you move them too soon.

## Variations

**Tahini chocolate chip:** Replace ½ cup butter with ½ cup tahini. Adds a rich, nutty, almost savory quality.

**Brown butter toffee:** Add 150g toffee bits with the chocolate chips.

**Dark chocolate sea salt:** Use only 70%+ dark chocolate (chopped into chunks, not chips). The irregular chocolate pieces create pools of melted chocolate in the final cookie.

**Walnut chocolate:** Add 100g toasted walnut pieces with the chocolate chips.

**Triple chocolate:** Use a mix of dark, milk, and white chocolate chips.

---

## Storage

**Room temperature:** Store in an airtight container with a slice of white bread (keeps cookies soft) for up to 5 days.

**Freeze baked cookies:** Cool completely, freeze in a single layer, then transfer to a zip-lock bag. Warm from frozen in a 300°F oven for 5–8 minutes or microwave 20 seconds.

**Freeze unbaked dough:** Scoop into balls and freeze on a sheet pan. Once frozen, transfer to a zip-lock bag. Bake from frozen at 375°F for **13–15 minutes**.

Frozen unbaked cookie dough is possibly the best thing you can keep in your freezer. Fresh cookies whenever you want them, in under 15 minutes.
