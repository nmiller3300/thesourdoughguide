There's something almost magical about a fresh soft pretzel. That deep mahogany crust, the satisfying chew, the way coarse salt crystals crunch between your teeth. Now imagine that pretzel with the subtle tang of sourdough running through every bite.

These sourdough soft pretzels are everything you want them to be — golden, chewy, slightly tangy, and absolutely irresistible warm from the oven. They're perfect for game day, after-school snacks, or honestly, just because it's Tuesday and you deserve something wonderful.

The sourdough discard adds complexity to the flavor and tenderness to the crumb, while the traditional baking soda bath gives you that signature pretzel crust. Let's make some magic.

## Yield

**8 large soft pretzels**

---

## Ingredients

### For the Dough

| Ingredient | Grams | Volume |
|------------|-------|--------|
| Warm water (100-105°F / 38-40°C) | 240g | 1 cup |
| Sourdough discard (unfed) | 113g | ½ cup |
| Brown sugar | 28g | 2 tablespoons |
| Fine sea salt | 9g | 1½ teaspoons |
| Unsalted butter, melted | 43g | 3 tablespoons |
| Bread flour | 450g | 3½ cups + 2 tablespoons |
| Instant yeast | 7g | 2¼ teaspoons |

### For the Baking Soda Bath

| Ingredient | Grams | Volume |
|------------|-------|--------|
| Water | 2 liters | 8 cups |
| Baking soda | 64g | ¼ cup |
| Brown sugar | 28g | 2 tablespoons |

### For Topping

| Ingredient | Grams | Volume |
|------------|-------|--------|
| Unsalted butter, melted | 28g | 2 tablespoons |
| Coarse pretzel salt or flaky sea salt | — | As needed |

## Equipment Needed

- Large mixing bowl or stand mixer
- Wide pot or Dutch oven for the baking soda bath
- Slotted spoon or spider strainer
- Two baking sheets lined with parchment paper
- Pastry brush

---

## Method

### 1. Make the Dough (10 minutes active, 1 hour rising)

In a large bowl or the bowl of a stand mixer, combine the warm water, sourdough discard, brown sugar, and melted butter. Stir until the sugar dissolves.

Add the flour, salt, and instant yeast. Mix until a shaggy dough forms. If using a stand mixer with a dough hook, knead on medium-low speed for 6-7 minutes. If kneading by hand, turn the dough onto a lightly floured surface and knead for 8-10 minutes.

The dough is ready when it's smooth, slightly tacky but not sticky, and springs back when poked. It should feel firm and almost playdough-like — this is normal and necessary for shaping.

Shape the dough into a ball and place it in a lightly oiled bowl. Cover with a damp towel or plastic wrap and let rise at room temperature until doubled in size, about 1 to 1½ hours. If your kitchen is cool, this may take closer to 2 hours.

### 2. Divide and Shape (20 minutes)

Once the dough has doubled, turn it onto a clean work surface. You shouldn't need flour — the dough should be smooth and workable.

Divide the dough into 8 equal pieces. I recommend using a scale here — each piece should weigh about 110g for uniform pretzels.

Work with one piece at a time, keeping the others covered. Roll each piece into a rope about 22-24 inches long. Start in the center and work outward, applying gentle, even pressure. The rope should be slightly thinner in the middle and a bit thicker at the ends.

To shape the pretzel: Holding the ends of the rope, lift and cross them over each other to form a loop with the ends pointing toward you. Twist the ends around each other once, then bring them down and press them firmly onto the bottom curve of the pretzel. The twist should sit at the top of the pretzel shape.

Place shaped pretzels on your lined baking sheets, leaving 2 inches between each. Cover loosely and let rest for 15-20 minutes while you prepare the baking soda bath and preheat your oven.

### 3. Prepare the Baking Soda Bath (5 minutes)

Preheat your oven to 425°F (220°C).

In a wide pot or Dutch oven, bring 8 cups of water to a gentle boil. Add the baking soda and brown sugar, stirring until dissolved. Reduce heat to maintain a steady simmer.

**Important:** The baking soda will bubble up when added — use a pot with plenty of headroom and add it gradually.

### 4. Boil the Pretzels (10 minutes)

Working with 2-3 pretzels at a time, gently lower them into the simmering baking soda bath. Boil for 30 seconds per side, flipping carefully with a slotted spoon or spider strainer.

Lift each pretzel from the bath, letting excess water drip off for a moment, then return it to the parchment-lined baking sheet.

Repeat with remaining pretzels.

The pretzels will look slightly deflated and have a tacky, almost slimy surface. This is exactly right — that alkaline coating is what creates the gorgeous dark crust and distinctive pretzel flavor.

### 5. Score and Season (2 minutes)

Using a sharp knife or razor blade, make a 3-4 inch horizontal slash across the thickest part of each pretzel, about ¼ inch deep. This isn't just decorative — it helps the pretzel expand properly in the oven.

Sprinkle generously with coarse pretzel salt or flaky sea salt while the surface is still tacky.

### 6. Bake (14-16 minutes)

Bake the pretzels at 425°F (220°C) for 14-16 minutes, rotating the pans halfway through. They're done when deeply golden brown — don't pull them out too early, or you'll miss that signature pretzel color and flavor.

The moment they come out of the oven, brush generously with melted butter. This adds flavor and gives them an irresistible shine.

### 7. Serve Immediately

Soft pretzels are best eaten warm, within a few hours of baking. Serve with your favorite dipping sauce and prepare for them to disappear.

## Tips for Perfect Sourdough Pretzels

**Don't skip the baking soda bath.** This step is non-negotiable. The alkaline solution is what transforms regular bread dough into a true pretzel, creating that characteristic chewy crust and deep brown color. Water alone won't cut it, and neither will a quick brush of baking soda solution.

**Keep the dough firm.** Pretzel dough is drier than most bread doughs for good reason — it needs to hold its shape during boiling. Resist the urge to add more water if it feels stiff. A 65-68% hydration dough is ideal here.

**Roll evenly for consistent texture.** Thicker areas will be softer and chewier, while thinner sections will be crispier. If you like variety in each bite, that's fine. If you prefer consistency, take your time rolling even ropes.

## Storage Instructions

Soft pretzels are truly best the day they're made. However, you have options:

**Room temperature:** Store in an airtight container for up to 2 days. Reheat in a 350°F oven for 5-7 minutes to refresh.

**Freezing:** Freeze baked and cooled pretzels in a single layer, then transfer to a freezer bag. They'll keep for up to 2 months. Reheat from frozen in a 375°F oven for 10-12 minutes.

**Freezing unbaked:** Shape pretzels and freeze before boiling. When ready to bake, let them thaw for 30 minutes, then proceed with the baking soda bath and baking.

## Variations

### Cinnamon Sugar Pretzels

Omit the coarse salt topping. After baking, brush generously with melted butter and immediately toss in a mixture of ½ cup granulated sugar and 2 teaspoons ground cinnamon. Serve with cream cheese frosting for dipping.

### Everything Pretzels

Replace the pretzel salt with everything bagel seasoning (sesame seeds, poppy seeds, dried garlic, dried onion, and flaky salt). Brush with butter after baking as usual.

### Cheese-Stuffed Pretzels

Before shaping, flatten each dough piece slightly and place a 1-ounce stick of mozzarella or cheddar in the center. Roll the dough around the cheese, seal the edges, then roll into a rope and shape as usual. The cheese will be melty and perfect inside.

### Pretzel Bites

Instead of shaping traditional pretzels, roll each piece into a 12-inch rope and cut into 1-inch pieces. Boil in batches for 30 seconds total (no flipping needed), then bake for 10-12 minutes. Perfect for dipping.
