# The Poke Test — How to Know When Your Sourdough Is Perfectly Proofed

You've mixed your dough. You've folded it with care. You've shaped it beautifully and tucked it into its proofing basket. Now comes the question that haunts every sourdough baker: *Is it ready?*

This is where the poke test becomes your best friend.

## What Is the Poke Test?

The poke test is exactly what it sounds like — a gentle finger poke that tells you how fermented your dough is. It's low-tech, requires zero equipment, and once you understand what you're feeling, it becomes remarkably reliable.

Here's the principle: as dough ferments, the gluten structure relaxes and gas bubbles accumulate. The way dough responds to pressure changes predictably as fermentation progresses. By poking your dough and watching how it reacts, you can read its internal state without cutting it open or relying solely on timing.

## How to Perform the Poke Test

1. **Lightly flour your finger.** Just a dusting — you don't want the dough sticking to you, but you also don't want to leave a pile of flour on your loaf.

2. **Poke the dough gently but deliberately.** Use your index finger and press about 1/2 inch (1.25 cm) into the dough. Don't jab it aggressively, but don't be too timid either. You need enough pressure to create a real indentation.

3. **Remove your finger and watch.**

4. **Observe how the dough responds over 2-3 seconds.**

## Reading the Results

### Springs Back Quickly and Completely
The indentation disappears almost immediately, like poking a balloon. **Your dough is underproofed.** The gluten is still tight, and there's not enough gas development. Give it more time — anywhere from 30 minutes to 2 hours depending on your environment.

### Springs Back Slowly and Mostly
The indentation fills in gradually over 2-3 seconds, but a slight impression remains visible. **This is the sweet spot.** Your dough is properly proofed and ready for the oven. The gluten has relaxed enough to be extensible but retains enough strength to spring back.

### Stays Indented With Little to No Spring Back
The poke mark just sits there, looking sad and deflated. **Your dough is overproofed.** The gluten structure has weakened too much, and much of the gas has escaped or the yeast has exhausted its food supply. You can still bake it, but expect a denser crumb and less oven spring.

## When to Use the Poke Test

The poke test works best during **final proof** — after shaping, when your dough is in its banneton or proofing container. This is the critical moment when timing matters most.

You can also use it during bulk fermentation to gauge progress, though the dough will feel different since it hasn't been shaped and degassed yet. During bulk, you're looking for the dough to feel airier and more pillowy overall.

For final proof specifically:
- **Room temperature proof (70-75°F / 21-24°C):** Start checking after 2 hours
- **Cold retard in the refrigerator:** The poke test still works on cold dough, though the response will be slightly slower. Check before it goes in, and check again before baking.

## Common Mistakes

### Poking Too Hard
If you jab your dough like you're angry at it, you'll deflate the surface and get a misleading reading. The poke should be firm but gentle — think of pressing a ripe peach to check if it's ready.

### Testing Too Often
Every poke leaves a mark and releases a tiny bit of gas. Once or twice during final proof is plenty. Constantly poking your dough is like opening the oven door every five minutes — it disrupts the process.

### Ignoring Visual Cues
The poke test works best alongside other observations. Is your dough noticeably puffier than when you shaped it? Does it feel lighter when you lift the banneton? Does it jiggle slightly when you move it? These visual and tactile cues confirm what the poke test tells you.

### Forgetting About Temperature
A dough proofing at 78°F (26°C) will reach the "ready" stage much faster than one at 65°F (18°C). The poke test doesn't replace understanding your environment — it works with that knowledge.

### Expecting Identical Results Every Time
Different flours, hydration levels, and dough compositions respond slightly differently. A high-hydration dough will feel softer and may not spring back as assertively even when perfectly proofed. A stiffer, lower-hydration dough will always feel firmer. Learn what "ready" feels like for *your* typical recipe.

## Practical Takeaways

- **The ideal response:** Dough springs back slowly over 2-3 seconds, leaving a slight indentation
- **Underproofed:** Springs back immediately and completely — needs more time
- **Overproofed:** Stays indented with no spring — bake it now and adjust timing next time
- **Flour your finger lightly** to prevent sticking
- **Combine with visual cues** like increased volume and a jiggly texture
- **Practice builds intuition** — the more you poke, the better you'll read your dough

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use the poke test on dough straight from the refrigerator?
Yes, but adjust your expectations. Cold dough is stiffer and responds more slowly, so the spring-back will be less dramatic overall. What you're looking for is *some* spring-back rather than none. If your cold dough feels completely slack and doesn't respond at all, it's likely overproofed. Many bakers bake straight from the fridge, so learning how your cold dough should feel is valuable.

### What if my dough passes the poke test but doesn't rise much in the oven?
The poke test confirms fermentation state, but oven spring depends on other factors too: steam, oven temperature, scoring, and gluten strength. If your poke test was accurate but you got minimal rise, investigate those other variables. Weak gluten development during bulk fermentation is a common culprit.

### How accurate is the poke test compared to other methods?
The poke test is surprisingly reliable once you calibrate your expectations to your specific dough. Some bakers prefer volume measurements (waiting for dough to increase by 50-75%), and others use the aliquot jar method for precise tracking. The poke test's advantage is speed and simplicity — no equipment, no calculations, just you and your dough having a conversation.

## The Bigger Picture

The poke test isn't magic, and it isn't foolproof. It's a tool — one that gets sharper with every loaf you bake. The first few times, you might second-guess yourself. That's normal.

But here's what happens over time: you start to *feel* the difference between tight, underproofed dough and relaxed, ready dough. You notice how the surface looks different, how the whole mass jiggles when you move the basket. The poke test becomes just one part of a larger sensory conversation with your bread.

That intuition? It's the thing that separates nervous bakers from confident ones. And it starts with a single, gentle poke.
