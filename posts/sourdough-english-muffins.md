# Sourdough English Muffins

The nooks and crannies in a proper English muffin aren't an accident — they're created by a soft, active dough and the right cooking technique. These sourdough English muffins have the real thing: irregular holes that catch melted butter and hold it hostage, a slightly chewy interior, and a toasted-cornmeal crust.

They're cooked entirely on a griddle or cast iron skillet — no oven required (well, almost). The result is dramatically better than anything from a grocery store.

## Ingredients

*(Makes 10–12 muffins)*

- **450g** all-purpose flour
- **200g** warm whole milk
- **100g** warm water
- **150g** active sourdough starter
- **15g** honey or sugar
- **8g** fine sea salt
- **20g** unsalted butter, softened
- **Cornmeal** for dusting (essential — creates the characteristic texture on the outside)

---

## Method

### Day 1 — Mix and Overnight Ferment

1. Combine milk, water, starter, and honey in a large bowl. Add flour and salt. Mix until a soft, slightly sticky dough forms.

2. Add softened butter and work it into the dough by squeezing and folding.

3. The dough should be softer than bread dough but not wet — somewhere between sticky and tacky. **Resist adding more flour.**

4. Cover and ferment at room temperature **3–5 hours** until about 50% increased.

5. **Refrigerate overnight** (8–16 hours).

---

### Day 2 — Shape, Rest, Cook

**Remove from fridge 30 minutes before shaping.**

**Shape the muffins:**
There are two methods — use whichever feels easier.

*Method A (rounds):*
1. Turn dough onto a cornmeal-dusted surface.
2. Pat gently to about ¾ inch thickness (don't use a rolling pin — you'll deflate it).
3. Cut rounds with a 3–3.5 inch biscuit cutter or the rim of a glass.
4. Re-roll scraps once.

*Method B (balls):*
1. Divide dough into 10–12 equal pieces (about 80g each).
2. Shape each into a smooth ball.
3. Flatten gently to about ¾ inch thickness.

**Dust generously with cornmeal on both sides.** Place on a cornmeal-dusted baking sheet, spacing them apart.

**Rest 1–2 hours** at room temperature until slightly puffed. They won't look dramatically bigger — that's fine.

**Cook on the griddle:**
1. Heat a cast iron skillet or griddle over **low-medium heat**. No butter or oil needed.
2. The heat level is critical: too hot and the outside burns before the inside cooks; too low and they don't develop color. Aim for a surface that takes **7–8 minutes per side** to achieve golden color.
3. Test with one muffin first. If it's golden at 7 minutes, proceed. Adjust heat accordingly.
4. Cook muffins **7–8 minutes per side** until golden brown on both sides. Work in batches — don't crowd the pan.

**Finish in the oven (optional but recommended):**
If you're unsure they're cooked through — especially for thick ones — transfer to a 350°F oven for **5–8 minutes** after the griddle. Internal temp should reach 190°F.

**Cool 10–15 minutes** before splitting.

---

## The Fork Split

**Never use a knife to cut English muffins.** Use a fork to poke around the equator of the muffin, creating a perforation, then pull the two halves apart with your hands. This technique preserves the irregular interior structure that creates nooks and crannies. A knife cuts through those holes and leaves you with a flat, smooth surface that doesn't hold butter.

## Serving

**Classic:** Toasted + real butter + good jam  
**Eggs Benedict:** Toasted muffin + Canadian bacon + poached egg + hollandaise  
**Breakfast sandwich:** Fried egg + sharp cheddar + hot sauce  
**Pizza muffin:** Tomato sauce + mozzarella + toppings → broil 3 minutes  
**Simple:** Toasted + cream cheese + smoked salmon + everything seasoning

## Storage

**Room temperature:** 3–4 days in a zip-lock bag or bread bag.

**Refrigerator:** Up to 1 week (refrigeration keeps them from molding, though the texture is best at room temperature).

**Freezer:** Excellent frozen. Split before freezing. Toast directly from frozen — 3–4 minutes in a toaster. Frozen sourdough English muffins might be one of the best things to keep in your freezer.

---

## Troubleshooting

**Gummy inside:** Undercooked. Finish in the oven at 350°F until internal temp reaches 190°F.

**Burnt outside, raw inside:** Heat too high. Lower the temperature and cook longer.

**No nooks and crannies:** Dough was too stiff (added too much flour) or overhandled. Be gentle and keep it soft.

**Spreading flat:** Dough was over-fermented or too warm. Chill dough briefly before cutting next time.
