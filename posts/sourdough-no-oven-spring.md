# No Oven Spring? Here's Why Your Sourdough Isn't Rising in the Oven

You shaped it. You proofed it. You preheated your oven. You slid that beautiful scored loaf into the Dutch oven — and then you lifted the lid 20 minutes later to find something that looks more like a frisbee than bread.

Flat loaves are one of the most discouraging failures in sourdough baking. But they're almost always fixable, because they almost always come down to one of five causes.

## What Actually Causes Oven Spring

Oven spring — the dramatic rise that happens in the first 10–15 minutes of baking — requires three things working together:

1. **Active yeast** producing CO₂ that expands rapidly in the heat
2. **Elastic gluten** that stretches to contain that expanding gas
3. **A scoring cut** that channels the expansion in a controlled direction

If any one of these fails, your loaf will be flat. Here's how to diagnose which one you're dealing with.

---

## Cause 1: Your Starter Wasn't Active Enough

This is the most common cause, especially for newer bakers. Using starter that isn't at peak activity means you're putting yeast into your dough that can't produce enough gas to drive a rise.

**How to tell:** Your starter doesn't reliably double within 4–6 hours of feeding, doesn't dome on top, or fails the float test.

**The fix:**
- Feed your starter 4–8 hours before mixing dough, in a warm spot (75–80°F)
- Use it when it has doubled, looks domed and bubbly, and a spoonful dropped in water floats
- If it won't pass the float test, don't bake with it that day — feed it again and wait

---

## Cause 2: Your Dutch Oven Wasn't Hot Enough

A cold or lukewarm Dutch oven is responsible for more flat loaves than almost anything else. The cast iron needs to be ripping hot for the initial blast of heat to trigger rapid oven spring.

**How to tell:** You preheated the Dutch oven for less than 40 minutes, or your oven runs cool.

**The fix:**
- Preheat your oven and Dutch oven together at 500°F for **at least 45–60 minutes**
- Get an oven thermometer — many ovens run 15–25°F cooler than the dial shows
- Never put cold dough into a Dutch oven that hasn't been properly preheated

---

## Cause 3: The Dough Was Over-Proofed

Over-proofed dough has fermented so long that the gluten structure has degraded. The gas that was supposed to expand in the oven has already mostly escaped. What's left can't hold the structure up.

**How to tell:** The dough feels very slack and fragile when you go to score it. It doesn't hold its shape well. The poke test shows no spring-back at all.

**The fix:**
- Use the poke test before baking: properly proofed dough springs back slowly, about halfway. No spring at all means overproofed.
- Monitor volume during bulk — 50–75% increase is the target, not double
- In warm kitchens (above 75°F), bulk fermentation happens much faster than recipe times suggest

---

## Cause 4: Your Score Was Too Shallow or Hesitant

The score line is a deliberately weak point that lets the loaf expand upward in a controlled way. If it's too shallow, the gas finds a different weak point — a seam, a crack, the side of the loaf — and that's where it escapes instead.

**How to tell:** The loaf is flat but has a side burst or random crack.

**The fix:**
- Score at least ½ inch deep at a 30–45° angle in one swift, confident motion
- Use a sharp lame or fresh razor blade — a dull blade drags rather than cuts
- Score cold dough straight from the fridge for the cleanest, most precise cuts

---

## Cause 5: Insufficient Steam During the Bake

Without steam, the crust sets too early and becomes a rigid shell that physically prevents the loaf from expanding. This is why the Dutch oven method works so well — it traps steam from the dough itself.

**How to tell:** Crust looks dried out and set very early in the bake.

**The fix:**
- Always bake covered for the first 20 minutes — this is where oven spring happens
- Make sure your Dutch oven lid fits tightly and there are no significant gaps
- Don't open the oven during the covered phase

---

## Quick Checklist Before Your Next Bake

Before loading the dough, run through this list:

- [ ] Starter doubled within 4–6 hours of feeding and passed the float test
- [ ] Dutch oven preheating for at least 45 minutes at 500°F
- [ ] Poke test shows slow, partial spring-back (not complete, not zero)
- [ ] Sharp blade ready for scoring
- [ ] Planning to bake covered for a full 20 minutes

If all five are checked, you'll get oven spring. The flat loaf era is over.
