# Sourdough Pizza Dough

If you make sourdough bread, you have everything you need to make exceptional pizza. The starter adds flavor complexity, the long cold fermentation develops chewiness, and baking at maximum oven temperature creates the crispy-bottomed, bubbled crust that separates great pizza from good pizza.

This dough makes 3 pizzas and gets better the longer it ferments — up to 72 hours in the fridge.

## Ingredients

*(Makes 3 × 280g dough balls — enough for 3 individual pizzas)*

- **600g** bread flour (or 00 flour for more authentic Neapolitan texture)
- **390g** water (65% hydration — lower than bread for a more manageable pizza dough)
- **120g** active sourdough starter
- **14g** fine sea salt
- **20g** olive oil

---

## Method

### Mix the Dough
1. Combine flour and 370g water. Mix until no dry flour remains. Rest 30 minutes (autolyse).
2. Add starter, remaining 20g water, salt, and olive oil. Mix vigorously for 5 minutes until fully incorporated.
3. The dough should feel smooth and slightly tacky.

### Develop Strength
Perform **3 sets of stretch and folds** over 1.5 hours. Pizza dough doesn't need as much development as bread — you're not trying to trap gas for oven spring, you're building elasticity for stretching.

### Bulk Ferment
Leave covered at room temperature **4–6 hours** until noticeably puffy and slightly domed.

### Divide and Refrigerate
1. Turn dough onto a lightly oiled surface.
2. Divide into **3 equal pieces** (approximately 280g each).
3. Shape each piece into a tight ball by pulling the edges underneath and rolling it against the counter to build surface tension.
4. Place each ball in a lightly oiled container or a floured tray, cover tightly, and **refrigerate 24–72 hours**.

The longer the cold ferment, the better the flavor. 48–72 hours is the sweet spot.

---

## Baking Your Pizza

### Equipment
A **pizza stone, baking steel, or heavy baking sheet** preheated in the oven makes the critical difference. The crust needs immediate, intense bottom heat to crisp properly before the toppings burn.

### Preheat — This Step Cannot Be Rushed
Set oven to **max temperature** (usually 500–550°F / 260–290°C). Place stone or steel on the **lowest rack**. Preheat for **minimum 45 minutes** — ideally 1 hour.

### Shape the Dough
1. Remove dough from fridge **30–60 minutes** before baking.
2. Dust your work surface generously with flour or semolina.
3. Flatten the dough ball gently with your fingers, then use your hands to stretch it outward. **Do not use a rolling pin** — it degasses the dough and kills the texture.
4. Work from the center outward, letting gravity help by draping the dough over your knuckles. 
5. Stretch to **10–12 inches** in diameter. It doesn't need to be perfectly round.

### Assemble and Bake
1. Transfer shaped dough to a floured pizza peel or parchment paper.
2. Add sauce (keep it thin — less is more), cheese, and toppings.
3. **Work quickly** — the longer toppings sit on raw dough, the soggier it gets.
4. Slide onto hot stone/steel. Bake **8–12 minutes** until crust is charred in spots, cheese is bubbly, and the underside is deep golden.

## Sauce Recipe (Simple)

This is all you need:

- 1 can (400g) San Marzano or good-quality crushed tomatoes
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- Salt to taste
- Fresh basil (torn in after baking, not before)

Don't cook the sauce. Just mix cold, spread thin. Heat from the oven does the rest.

---

## Topping Ideas

**Margherita:** Crushed tomatoes, fresh mozzarella, basil after baking, good olive oil  
**White pizza:** Olive oil base, ricotta, mozzarella, garlic, lemon zest, arugula after baking  
**Pepperoni:** Sauce, low-moisture mozzarella, pepperoni (cup-and-char style if you can find it)  
**Mushroom:** Olive oil base, gruyère, sautéed mushrooms, fresh thyme, truffle oil after baking  

---

## Storing Extra Dough Balls

Refrigerate for up to **4 days**. Freeze for up to **3 months** — freeze after the cold ferment, thaw overnight in the fridge, and use within 24 hours of thawing.
