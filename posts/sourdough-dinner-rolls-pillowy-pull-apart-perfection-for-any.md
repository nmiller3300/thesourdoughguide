There's something about a basket of warm dinner rolls that transforms a meal. They turn a Tuesday night chicken dinner into an event. They make Thanksgiving feel complete. And when those rolls are made with sourdough? You get all that comfort plus a depth of flavor that store-bought simply cannot touch.

These sourdough dinner rolls are everything you want them to be: soft enough to pull apart with a satisfying tear, tender enough to practically melt in your mouth, with just enough tang to remind you they're homemade. The golden tops gleam with butter, and the insides are pillowy clouds that beg to be slathered with more butter, honey, or dragged through gravy.

The best part? They're far more forgiving than a traditional sourdough loaf. The enriched dough is easier to handle, and the smaller size means faster proofing and baking. Whether you're a seasoned sourdough baker or just getting comfortable with your starter, these rolls will reward you.

## Yield

**12 dinner rolls** (can be doubled for a larger gathering)

---

## Ingredients

### For the Dough

| Ingredient | Grams | Volume |
|------------|-------|--------|
| Active sourdough starter (100% hydration) | 100g | Generous ⅓ cup |
| Whole milk, warm (90-95°F / 32-35°C) | 180g | ¾ cup |
| Unsalted butter, softened | 57g | 4 tablespoons |
| Honey | 28g | 1½ tablespoons |
| Egg, large, room temperature | 50g | 1 egg |
| All-purpose flour | 400g | 3¼ cups |
| Fine sea salt | 8g | 1½ teaspoons |

### For the Butter Wash

| Ingredient | Grams | Volume |
|------------|-------|--------|
| Unsalted butter, melted | 28g | 2 tablespoons |
| Flaky sea salt (optional) | — | Pinch |

---

## Equipment Needed

- 9x13 inch baking pan or two 9-inch round cake pans
- Stand mixer with dough hook (or strong arms)
- Kitchen scale
- Plastic wrap or damp towel
- Pastry brush

---

## Method

### Step 1: Mix the Dough (10 minutes)

In the bowl of a stand mixer, combine the warm milk, active starter, honey, and egg. Whisk gently to combine.

Add the flour and salt. Using the dough hook on low speed, mix until a shaggy dough forms, about 2 minutes. Let it rest for 5 minutes—this brief autolyse helps the flour hydrate.

With the mixer on medium-low, add the softened butter one tablespoon at a time, waiting until each piece is mostly incorporated before adding the next. This takes patience, about 3-4 minutes total.

Once all butter is added, increase speed to medium and knead for 6-8 minutes. The dough should become smooth, slightly tacky, and pull away from the bowl sides cleanly. It will pass the windowpane test—you should be able to stretch a small piece thin enough to see light through without it tearing.

### Step 2: Bulk Fermentation (4-6 hours)

Transfer the dough to a lightly greased bowl. Cover with plastic wrap or a damp towel.

Let it rise at room temperature (70-75°F / 21-24°C) until doubled in size, typically 4-6 hours depending on your starter strength and kitchen temperature.

The dough is ready when it's puffy, domed, and jiggles slightly when you nudge the bowl. You'll see bubbles just beneath the surface when you gently lift the edge.

**Tip:** If your kitchen runs cool, place the bowl near your oven with the light on, or use a proofing box set to 75°F.

### Step 3: Divide and Pre-Shape (15 minutes)

Gently turn the dough onto a lightly floured surface. Deflate it gently—no aggressive punching, just a tender press.

Using a bench scraper and kitchen scale, divide the dough into 12 equal pieces, approximately 68g each. Precision here ensures even baking.

Working with one piece at a time, pre-shape each into a rough ball by tucking the edges underneath and rotating it against the counter using a cupped hand. The surface tension you create now makes final shaping easier.

Cover the pre-shaped pieces with a damp towel and let them rest for 10 minutes.

### Step 4: Final Shape (10 minutes)

Grease your 9x13 pan with butter or line with parchment.

Take each pre-shaped ball and shape it tighter: cup your hand over the dough and move it in small circles against an unfloured section of counter. The friction creates surface tension, pulling the dough into a smooth, taut ball.

Arrange the shaped rolls in the prepared pan in a 3x4 pattern, spacing them evenly (about 1 inch apart). They'll grow into each other during proofing, creating that beautiful pull-apart quality.

### Step 5: Final Proof (2-3 hours, or overnight cold retard)

Cover the pan loosely with plastic wrap or a damp towel.

Let the rolls proof at room temperature until they've nearly doubled and are touching each other. They should look puffy and feel like soft pillows when gently poked—the indent will spring back slowly but not completely.

This typically takes 2-3 hours at room temperature.

**For an overnight rise:** Cover the pan tightly and refrigerate for 8-12 hours. The next day, let the rolls sit at room temperature for 1-2 hours before baking. This cold retard deepens the flavor and fits better into busy schedules.

### Step 6: Bake (22-28 minutes)

Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C) at least 20 minutes before baking.

Remove the plastic wrap. Bake the rolls on the center rack for 22-28 minutes, until the tops are deep golden brown and the internal temperature reaches 190°F (88°C).

The edges should be golden and the center rolls should spring back when touched. If the tops brown too quickly, tent loosely with foil for the last 10 minutes.

### Step 7: Butter and Serve

The moment the rolls come out of the oven, brush them generously with melted butter. Watch it soak in and make those tops glisten.

Sprinkle with flaky sea salt if desired—it adds a lovely crunch and flavor pop.

Let the rolls cool in the pan for 5 minutes, then serve warm. They pull apart beautifully, revealing that soft, slightly stringy interior.

## Tips for Perfect Dinner Rolls

**Use a truly active starter.** These rolls rise faster than a traditional loaf because of the enrichments (butter, egg, honey), but they still rely on a healthy, active starter. Feed your starter 4-8 hours before mixing and use it at peak activity—domed, bubbly, and passing the float test.

**Don't skip the butter brushing.** That final brush of melted butter does triple duty: it adds flavor, creates shine, and keeps the crust soft. If you prefer a slightly firmer crust, skip the butter—but you'll be missing out.

**Room temperature ingredients matter.** Cold eggs or butter will shock the dough and slow fermentation. Take them out of the refrigerator an hour before you begin.

---

## Storage Instructions

**At room temperature:** Store in an airtight container or plastic bag for up to 3 days. They'll soften slightly but remain delicious.

**To refresh:** Wrap rolls in foil and warm in a 300°F (150°C) oven for 8-10 minutes. They'll taste almost fresh-baked.

**To freeze:** Cool completely, then freeze in a single layer on a baking sheet. Once frozen, transfer to a freezer bag. They keep for up to 3 months. Thaw at room temperature and refresh in the oven as above.

## Variations

### Garlic Herb Rolls

Add 2 teaspoons of dried Italian herbs and 1 teaspoon of garlic powder to the flour. For the butter wash, mix 2 tablespoons melted butter with 1 minced garlic clove and 1 tablespoon fresh chopped parsley. Brush immediately after baking.

### Honey Whole Wheat Rolls

Replace 100g of the all-purpose flour with whole wheat flour. Increase honey to 42g (2 tablespoons). The rolls will be slightly denser with a nuttier flavor—perfect alongside soups and stews.

### Cheesy Pull-Apart Rolls

After shaping, press a small cube of cheddar or mozzarella into the center of each roll before placing in the pan. Sprinkle the tops with shredded Parmesan before baking.

### Cinnamon Sugar Rolls

Increase honey to 42g. After baking and butter brushing, immediately sprinkle with a mixture of 2 tablespoons sugar and 1 teaspoon cinnamon. These become an unexpected sweet addition to breakfast or brunch.
