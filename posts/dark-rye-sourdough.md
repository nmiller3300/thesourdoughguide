# Dark Rye Sourdough

Rye sourdough is a completely different beast from wheat-based loaves, and it rewards bakers who embrace rather than fight its nature. It's dense, moist, and intensely flavored — nothing like the airy open crumb of a country loaf. But that density is a feature: a thin slice of good rye bread carries more flavor per bite than three slices of plain white.

This is a 50% rye loaf — enough rye for deep flavor and characteristic density, with enough bread flour to maintain some structure and make it workable.

## Ingredients

- **225g** dark rye flour
- **225g** bread flour
- **340g** water (75% hydration — rye is exceptionally thirsty)
- **100g** active rye starter (or regular starter fed with rye for 1–2 days prior)
- **10g** fine sea salt
- **15g** caraway seeds (classic — don't skip unless you genuinely dislike them)
- **15g** molasses (optional — deepens color and adds a subtle sweetness)

**Yield:** 1 loaf | **Total time:** 18–24 hours

---

## Understanding Rye

**Rye flour behaves differently from wheat flour** — dramatically so. Rye contains very little gluten-forming protein, and what protein it does have can't form the strong gluten networks you rely on in wheat baking. Instead, rye's structure comes from starches and gums (pentosans) that absorb large amounts of water and form a gel-like matrix.

This means:
- **No windowpane test** — rye dough won't stretch thin without tearing. This is fine.
- **Much stickier dough** — expected. Don't add extra flour.
- **No oven spring** — rye loaves don't spring up dramatically. They expand slowly and steadily.
- **Longer cooling time** — the starch structure in rye needs 4–8 hours (ideally overnight) to fully set before slicing. Cutting too soon produces a gummy, undercooked-looking crumb.
- **Faster fermentation** — rye feeds wild yeast very aggressively. Watch your dough.

## Method

### 1. Mix
Combine all ingredients and mix thoroughly. The dough will look more like a thick batter than a typical bread dough — this is correct at 50% rye. It should be very sticky and dense.

Use a dough scraper to fold the dough rather than kneading (kneading isn't effective with rye). Fold it over itself 8–10 times, rotating the bowl, until it comes together.

### 2. Fermentation
Because rye ferments quickly, this loaf uses a shorter bulk fermentation:

Cover and leave at room temperature **3–5 hours** at 72°F. The dough should look slightly puffier and have small bubbles throughout. It won't double — that's fine.

### 3. Shape and Pan
Rye dough is too wet and sticky to free-form. Use a **9×5 inch loaf pan**, generously greased.

1. Wet your hands and scrape the dough into the pan.
2. Smooth the top with wet hands or a wet spatula.
3. Sprinkle a few extra caraway seeds on top if desired.
4. Cover loosely and proof **2–4 hours** until the dough crowns slightly above the rim. It won't rise as dramatically as a wheat loaf.

### 4. Bake
Preheat oven to **475°F (245°C)**.

Score down the center with a sharp blade (wet the blade first to prevent sticking).

- **First 10 minutes:** 475°F — creates initial crust
- **Reduce to 375°F:** Bake another **45–55 minutes** until deeply browned
- Internal temperature should reach **205°F (96°C)**

### 5. Cool — This Is Critical
Turn out of the pan immediately and cool on a wire rack. **Do not cut for at least 4 hours. Overnight is better.**

Rye starch continues to gelatinize as the loaf cools. Cutting early gives you a gummy, underset crumb that looks raw even though it's fully cooked. It's the single most common rye mistake.

Wrap the cooled loaf tightly and rest it **24 hours** before cutting for the best crumb.

## Serving Rye Sourdough

Rye is built for open-faced sandwiches. Slice thin:

**Classic combinations:**
- Smoked salmon + cream cheese + dill + thin cucumber
- Good butter + radishes + flaky salt
- Smoked herring or sardines + mustard + pickled onion
- Aged cheddar + apple slices + honey
- Cream cheese + smoked salmon + everything bagel seasoning

**Scandinavian style (smørrebrød):**
Top with anything — egg salad, roast beef, pickled vegetables. The dense rye base holds toppings without going soggy.

---

## Storage

Rye sourdough keeps remarkably well — 5–7 days at room temperature, wrapped in a kitchen towel or bread bag. It also slices more cleanly as it ages (day 2 is often easier to slice than day 1).

Freezes beautifully: slice before freezing, toast from frozen.
