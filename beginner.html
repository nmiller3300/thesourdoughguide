The Beginner's Guide to Sourdough

From zero experience to your first beautiful loaf — everything you need, in order.

Step 1

What is Sourdough?

Sourdough is bread leavened with a fermented flour-and-water mixture called a starter — instead of commercial yeast. The starter is alive with wild yeast and bacteria that give sourdough its signature tangy flavor, chewy crumb, and thick crust.

Unlike regular bread, sourdough is slower. That slowness is the magic — longer fermentation develops flavor and makes the bread easier to digest.

Step 2

Building Your Starter

Your starter is the foundation of all sourdough baking. Here's the simple truth: a starter is just flour and water, left to ferment over several days until wild yeast colonizes it.

What you need: All-purpose or bread flour, water (room temp), a clean jar, and 7 days of patience.

Day-by-Day Starter Guide

Day 1

Mix 50g flour + 50g water in a jar. Stir well. Cover loosely. Leave at room temp.

Day 2

You may see a few bubbles. Discard half, add 50g flour + 50g water. Stir and cover.

Day 3

More activity! Repeat the discard and feed. It may smell a little funky — that's normal.

Day 4-5

Bubbles increasing. Keep feeding daily. The smell should start improving.

Day 6

Your starter should be doubling in size between feedings. Test the float test.

Day 7

If your starter passes the float test — drop a small spoonful in water, it floats if ready — you're good to bake!

Step 3

Your First Loaf

Once your starter is active, you're ready to bake. Here's a simple, reliable recipe for your first sourdough loaf.

Simple Sourdough — Ingredients

  • 450g bread flour
  • 325g water (room temp)
  • 90g active starter
  • 9g salt

Basic Timeline

  • Mix — Combine all ingredients, rest 30 min
  • Stretch & fold — 4 sets over 2 hours
  • Bulk ferment — 4–8 hours at room temp
  • Shape — Shape and place in a banneton
  • Cold proof — Overnight in the fridge
  • Bake — 500°F in Dutch oven, 20 min covered + 20 min uncovered
Step 4

Reading Your Results

Your first loaf won't be perfect — and that's fine. Every bake is data. Here's what to look for:

Good signs
  • Open crumb with holes
  • Thick, crackly crust
  • Ear on the score
  • Hollow when tapped
Common issues
  • Dense crumb → under-fermented
  • Flat loaf → weak starter
  • Gummy inside → under-baked
  • Too sour → over-fermented
